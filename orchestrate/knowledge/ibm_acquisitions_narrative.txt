IBM ACQUISITIONS AND DIVESTITURES (78 deals: 76 acquisitions, 2 divestitures; disclosed values exist for only 24 deals - IBM historically did not disclose most deal values). All values US$ millions.

1984 - ROLM Corporation (acquisition, major, category: Hardware / Telecom). Deal value: $1,300 million ($1.3 billion). Closed: 1984-11. Acquired ROLM, a maker of PBX telephone equipment and military computers. IBM sold ROLM to Siemens in 1988 after the business underperformed. IBM's own report language: 1984 annual report: 'amounts expended for the acquisition of the net assets of ROLM Corporation'

1995 - Lotus Development Corporation (acquisition, major, category: Software). Deal value: $2,880 million ($2.9 billion). Closed: 1995-07-05. Acquired Lotus for its Notes collaboration platform, which became IBM's foundation for enterprise messaging and collaboration software. IBM's own report language: 1995 cash flow statement direct line item

1996 - Tivoli Systems, Inc. (acquisition, major, category: Software). Deal value: $716 million. Closed: 1996-03-04. Acquired Tivoli to build IBM's systems management and IT operations software portfolio, now a core part of IBM Automation. IBM's own report language: 1996 cash flow statement direct line item

1999 - Sequent Computer Systems (acquisition, major, category: Hardware / Servers). Deal value: $810 million. Closed: 1999-09. Acquired Sequent for its NUMA (non-uniform memory access) parallel server technology, strengthening IBM's high-end UNIX server line. IBM's own report language: 1999 CEO letter: 'Sequent Computer Systems strengthens the high end of our Web server line'

2001 - Informix Database Business (acquisition, major, category: Software). Deal value: $1,000 million ($1.0 billion). Closed: 2001-07-01. Acquired Informix's database software business from IBM's rival, significantly expanding DB2 market share. The Informix database products were rebranded under the DB2 family. IBM's own report language: 2001 annual report: 'The bulk of our acquisition investment was used to acquire the database assets of Informix Corp.'

2002 - PricewaterhouseCoopers Consulting (acquisition, major, category: Consulting). Deal value: $3,500 million ($3.5 billion). Closed: 2002-10-01. Acquired PwC's consulting arm — 30,000 consultants — instantly creating one of the world's largest IT services businesses. Foundation of what is now IBM Consulting, generating $21B+ annually. IBM's own report language: 2003 CEO letter: 'PricewaterhouseCoopers Consulting, Rational software and 19 other companies in the past two years'

2003 - Rational Software (acquisition, major, category: Software). Deal value: $2,100 million ($2.1 billion). Closed: 2003-02-21. Acquired Rational for its software development tools (UML, requirements management, testing). Now IBM Engineering Lifecycle Management (ELM), used in aerospace, defense, and automotive. IBM's own report language: 2003 CEO letter reference

2005 - Ascential (acquisition, major). Deal value: $1,140 million ($1.1 billion).

2005 - PC Division → Lenovo (divestiture, major, category: Hardware / PCs). Deal value: $1,750 million ($1.8 billion). Closed: 2005-05-01. Sold the ThinkPad PC business to China's Lenovo for $1.75B. IBM retained an 18.9% stake in Lenovo. Strategic exit from commoditizing PC hardware to refocus on higher-margin enterprise technology. IBM's own report language: 2007 Acquisitions & Divestitures note — prior-year disclosure

2007 - DataMirror Corporation (acquisition, minor, category: Software). Deal value: $725 million. Closed: 2007-09-04. Acquired DataMirror for its real-time data replication and integration technology, enhancing IBM's data management portfolio. IBM's own report language: 2007 Acquisitions & Divestitures note

2008 - Cognos (acquisition, major, category: Software / Analytics). Deal value: $5,021 million ($5.0 billion). Closed: 2008-01-31. Acquired the world's leading business intelligence and performance management software company. Became the foundation of IBM Cognos Analytics, now part of IBM's AI & Analytics portfolio. IBM's own report language: 2008 Acquisitions & Divestitures note (Note E)
