International Business Machines Corporation 
To the Stockholders: 
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account 
for the year 1948, Consohdated Balance Sheet of the 
domestic companies and the Canadian subsidiary company 
as of December 31, 1948, the Auditor's Certificate, and 
a Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past 
Ten Years. 
Net Income 
The net income for the year of $28,100,534.82 compares 
with $23,553,951.88 for 1947, an increase of $4,546,582.94. 
The net income in 1948 equalled $11.21 per share on the 
2,506,712 shares of capital stock outstanding at the end 
of the period, and compares with $9.40 in 1947, if com-
puted on the same number of shares. 
In February 1948 the Corporation's capital stock was 
split up by issuing three-quarters of a share additional 
for each share held at the close of business on January 23, 
1948, which resulted in increasing the issued and out-
standing capital stock from 1,432,407 shares to 2,506,712 
shares. 
Dividends 
Cash dividends totaling $10,022,778.00 were paid during 
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of 
$1.00 per share. 
On October 26, 1948 a 5% stock dividend was declared, 
payable to stockholders of record at the close of business 
on January 7, 1949, and on January 28, 1949 a total of 
125,295 shares of capital stock were issued in payment of 
this stock dividend. 
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1949, 
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of 


===== PAGE 6 =====
shares, is the one hundred and thirty-sixth consecutive 
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company. 
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment 
During the year 1948, the Company's increased volume 
of business and replacement of obsolete equipment re-
quired an investment of $54,863,927.05 in rental machines 
and parts, factory and office equipment, and land and 
buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting 
to $12,243,450.34 was written off and charged against 
reserves provided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
On June 26, 1948 the Company's new factory building 
at Poughkeepsie, New York was dedicated, providing 
an additional 210,000 square feet of floor space to our 
manufacturing facilities at that location. 
Long-Term Indebtedness 
In order to supplement the profits, over and above cash 
dividends, which were reinvested in the business and to 
provide additional working capital for the expanded pro-
duction of rental equipment, the Corporation sold to The 
Prudential Insurance Company of America on July 19, 
1948 its 3% promissory note in the principal amount of 
$35,000,000.00, dated July 1, 1948, due July 1, 1968, at 
100% of such principal amount. This note, together with 
the Corporation's $30,000,000.00 Twelve-Year 23^% 
Debentures due August 1, 1958 and its $20,000,000.00 
Eighteen-Year 
Debentures due February 1, 1965, 
brought the total long-term indebtedness of the Corpora-
tion to $85,000,000.00, on which the average rate of 
interest is 2.65%. 
Investments 
The net assets of the Corporation and its subsidiaries 
located in Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, 
Hungary, Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan Countries 


===== PAGE 7 =====
continue to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet, 
pending more settled conditions in these countries. During 
the year 1948 the Corporation adjusted its investment in 
these countries by $659,255.07, based on latest available 
information, and added a similar amount to the Reserve 
for Foreign Investment. The Corporation has not included 
in its current income account any income from these 
countries not actually received in dollars. 
The foreign investment in "All Other Countries" in-
creased $2,196,515.97 during 1948 as a result of the current 
year's operations. 
Employees' Retirement Plans 
Payments made into the IBM Retirement Plans by 
the Corporation and its Canadian Subsidiary Company 
during 1948 totaled $2,625,132.84. Of this amount, 
$1,227,559.19 was charged to the previously established 
Reserve for Estimated Cost of Employees' Past Service 
under the Plans. This amount includes $670,094.10, the 
fourth payment to the Endicott Trust Company as 
Trustee under the IBM Retirement Plan, and a pre-
payment to the Trustee of $500,000.00 for 1949; the 
balance of $57,465.09 represents payment made by the 
Canadian Company for past service under the Canadian 
Company's Retirement Plan. The current year's premium 
of $1,352,573.65 paid to The Equitable Life Assurance 
Society of the United States under the IBM Plan for 
United States employees and the current year's cost of 
$45,000.00 under the Canadian Company's Plan were 
charged against income. 
Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan 
For the year 1948, the Board of Directors added 
$831,541.44 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the 
IBM Retirement Plan. This amount was determined by 


===== PAGE 8 =====
the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by Mr. 
Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation under 
his present employment contract as compared with his 
prior contract and, in accordance with its past practice, 
as recommended by Mr. Watson and approved by the 
Board of Directors in connection with Mr. Watson's 
voluntary reductions for each year 1942 to 1947, inclusive, 
the above amount was added to the Watson Fund for 
Supplementing the IBM Retirement Plan. The Fund, 
after taking into account interest income and benefit 
payments made during 1948, had a balance at December 
31, 1948 of $3,496,031.78. 
Unfilled Orders 
Although more of the Company's products were pro-
duced in 1948 than in any year in the history of the busi-
ness, unfilled orders at the end of the year were higher 
than at the end of any previous year, and production 
has been increased to meet the continued demand for 
our products. 
Research and Development 
The Company's Research and Engineering Laboratories 
continued their work during 1948 on projects relating to 
new inventions, and improvements of our existing 
products. New products added to the Company's regular 
lines in 1948 include alphabetical accounting machines 
with increased speed and greater capacity, a high speed 
sorter, an electronic calculating punch, an electronic time 
system and a new IBM electric typewriter. These new 
products incorporate the latest developments in the 
Company's long experience in developing and manufac-
turing accounting machines, time recording equipment, 
and all-electric typewriters.

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION: 
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and domestic and Canadian 
subsidiary companies as of December 31, 1948, and the related 
consolidated income and earned surplus account for the year then 
ended. Our examination was made in accordance with generally 
accepted auditing standards, and accordingly included such tests of 
the accounting records and such other auditing procedures as we 
considered necessary in the circumstances. 
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on 
the accounts of the Canadian subsidiary and the available financial 
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by 
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign 
subsidiaries and branches. 
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and 
consoHdated income and earned surplus account present fairly the 
financial position of the International Business Machines Corporation 
and domestic and Canadian subsidiary companies at December 31, 
1948 and the results of operations for the year then ended, in con-
formity with generally accepted accounting principles applied on a 
basis consistent with that of the preceding year.
