The net profit in 1943 after taxes equalled $8.85 per 
share on the 1,039,546 shares then outstanding. In 1944, 
the Company added 51,897 shares through a stock divi-
dend, making a total of 1,091,443 shares, on which the net 
profit after taxes for 1944 was $8.90 per share. 
The net profit for 1944 of $37,711,355.88 before taxes 
compares with $36,974,884.62 for the year 1943. 


===== PAGE 6 =====
Included in the total estimated taxes of $28,000,000.00 is 
$22,802,000.00 Excess Profits Taxes after deducting post-
war credits of $2,685,000.00. These credits are carried on 
the Balance Sheet under "Other Assets" pending payment 
hy the Government after the war, the total of such credits 
being $6,639,662.31 as of December 31, 1944. 
The net profit of $9,711,355.88 after taxes compares 
with $9,204,884.62 for 1943, an increase of $506,471.26. 
The net profit for the year 1944 is after provision for 
voluntary renegotiation of war contracts made with the 
United States Government computed in the same manner 
as was accepted by the Government for the year 1943. 
Cash dividends totaling $6,534,688.50 were paid during 
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of 
$1.50 per share. In addition, a stock dividend of 5 per cent 
(54,482 shares) was declared in November, 1944, and 
issued January 29, 1945, to stockholders of record Jan-
uary 15, 1945. 
In 1944 the Company added $1,000,000.00 from earnings 
to the Reserve for After War Adjustments and Contin-
gencies, bringing this Reserve to a total of $8,000,000.00 at 
December 31,1944. 
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in Decem-
ber, 1944, on the Corporation's holdings of 100,000 shares 
of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart Manufac-
turing Company. 
During 1944 there was invested $14,259,713.56 in Rental 
Machines, Plant and Equipment and Land and Buildings. 
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to $2,605,-
336.44 was written off and charged against reserves pro-
vided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
In addition to the manufacture of its regular products 
under priority regulations the Corporation continues in 


===== PAGE 7 =====
the manufacture of munitions for the United States Gov-
ernment. During 1944 the men and women of the Corpora-
tion's main plant at Endicott, New York, received, for 
the third and fourth time, the Army-Navy Production 
award for outstanding achievement in the production of 
war materials, and the Corporation's plant at Pough-
keepsie received this same award for the second and third 
time. 
During 1944 our Research and Engineering Departments 
worked on 48 different important projects for the U. S. 
Army, Navy and Air Forces. Satisfactory progress also 
was made in carrying on improvements in the Company's 
regular products as well as in new developments for the 
post-war period. 
As of this date, 32 per cent of our men are serving in the 
armed forces of the United States. Of these men in service, 
22 per cent are commissioned officers. Our women likewise 
are serving in every branch of service, there being 149 in 
service as of this date. We extend our deepest sympathies 
to the families of the sixty-four of our men who have given 
their lives for their country. 
A notice, proxy statement and form of proxy for the 
annual meeting of stockholders to be held April 24, 1945, 
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 2, 1945. 
By Order of the Board of Directors, 
THOMAS J. WATSON, 
President.

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION: 
We have examined the Consolidated Balance Sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian 
Subsidiary Companies as of December 31, 1944 and the Consolidated 
Income and Earned Surplus Account for the year then ended. We 
have reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting pro-
cedures of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed 
audit of the transactions, have examined accounting records of the 
domestic companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we 
deemed appropriate. 
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on 
the accounts of the Canadian Subsidiary and the available 
financial 
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by 
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches located in countries other than liberated, enemy 
controlled and countries where exchange is blocked. Our examination 
was made in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards 
applicable in the circumstances and included all procedures which we 
considered necessary, except that it was impracticable to obtain con-
firmation on all accounts due from the United States Government but 
we satisfied ourselves by other means as to the substantial accuracy 
of such accounts. 
In our opinion, the accompanying Consolidated Balance Sheet and 
related Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account fairly 
present the financial condition of the International Business Ma-
chines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Com-
panies as at December 31, 1944 and the results of operations for the 
year ended on that date, in conformity with generally accepted 
accounting principles applied on a basis consistent with that of the 
preceding year.
