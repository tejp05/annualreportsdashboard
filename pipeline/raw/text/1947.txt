To the Stockholders: 
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account 
for the year 1947, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the 
domestic companies and the Canadian subsidiary company 
as of December 31, 1947, the Auditor's Certificate, and a 
Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past Ten 
Years. 
The results of the year's operations were as follows: 
Net Profit

Net Profit for the year 
$23,553,951.88 
The net profit for the year of $23,553,951.88 compares 
with $18,766,433.85 for 1946, an increase of $4,787,-
518.03. The net profit in 1947 equalled $16.44 per share 
on the 1,432,407 shares of capital stock outstanding, and 
compares with $13.10 in 1946 on the same number of 
shares. 
Cash Dividends 
Cash dividends totaling $8,589,738.00 were paid during 
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of 
$1.50 per share. In February 1948 the Corporation's 
capital stock was split up by issuing three-quarters of a 
share additional for each share held at the close of business 
on January 23,1948, which resulted in increasing the issued 
and outstanding capital stock from 1,432,407 shares to 
2,506,712 shares. The quarterly cash dividend payable 
March 10, 1948, at the rate of $1.00 per share on the in-
creased number of shares, represents an increase of 16^% 
in the amount of the cash dividend. The March 10, 1948 


===== PAGE 6 =====
dividend is the one hundred and thirty-second consecutive 
quarterly cash dividend. 
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment 
The Company's increased volume of business and re-
placement of obsolete and worn-out equipment required 
an investment of $38,112,622.19 in rental machines and 
parts, factory and office equipment, and land and build-
ings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to 
$10,051,078.16 was written off and charged against re-
serves provided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
Debenture Issues 
In order to provide funds for expansion, in addition to 
the amount available from the reinvestment of profits, 
in February 1947 the Corporation effected the sale at par 
to The Prudential Insurance Company of America of 
$20,000,000.00 Eighteen-Year 2^% Debentures due Feb-
ruary 1, 1965. These Debentures, together with the 
Corporation's $30,000,000.00 of Twelve-Year 
De-
bentures due August 1, 1958, which were outstanding as 
of December 31, 1946 and are held by the same insurance 
company, brought the total bonded indebtedness of the 
Corporation to $50,000,000.00. 
Investments 
The net assets of the Corporation and its subsidiaries 
located in Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, 
Hungary, Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan countries 
continue to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet, due 
to the unsettled conditions in these countries; but the 
Corporation has adjusted its investment in accordance 
with information received from these countries. The 
adjustment in 1947 amounted to $1,582,913.04, which 
was charged to the Reserve for Foreign Investment pro-
vided in prior years. Final valuation of the Corporation's 
investment in these countries cannot be determined until 
the present uncertainties are clarified and representative 


===== PAGE 7 =====
foreign exchange rates become established. The Corpora-
tion has not included in its current income account any 
profits or income on the investment in these countries 
not actually received in dollars. The foreign investment 
in "All Other Countries" increased $4,246,236.49 during 
1947 as a result of the current year's operations. 
In May 1934, the Company sold and transferred 
$4,141,566.83 of net current assets, plant and equipment, 
and patents and goodwill of its Dayton Scale Division to 
The Hobart Manufacturing Company for 100,000 shares 
of the latter's "Class B" capital stock. Since the date of 
sale, cash dividends aggregating $2,208,333.40 have been 
received. In connection with the recapitalization of The 
Hobart Manufacturing Company in December 1947, the 
Company exchanged its holdings of 100,000 shares of "Class 
B" stock for cash in the amount of $2,750,000.00 and 41,667 
shares of the new common stock of that Company. In 
order to carry the new stock at its market value at De-
cember 31, 1947, of $750,006.00 (included on the Balance 
Sheet in "Other Investments"), the difference of $641,-
560.83 was charged against current income in 1947. 
Employees' Retirement Plan 
During 1947, payments made by the Corporation into 
the IBM Retirement Plan totaled $1,804,192.82. Of this, 
$719,519.10, representing the third payment to theEndicott 
Trust Company as Trustee under the Plan, was charged 
to the previously established Reserve for Estimated Cost 
of Employees' Past Service under the IBM Retirement 
Plan; and $1,084,673.72, the current year's premium paid 
to The Equitable Life Assurance Society of the United 
States, was charged against income. On June 1, 1947 the 
IBM Co., Ltd. of Canada Retirement Plan was announced 
to employees. The amount of $517,185.81, transferred 
from Earned Surplus to Reserve for Estimated Cost of 
Employees' Past Service, represents the estimated past 
service credit under the Plan at December 31, 1947.

Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan 
Mr. Watson requested that the rate of the percentage 
compensation in his employment contract effective Janu-
ary 1,1947 be reduced from the 23^2% rate provided in his 
prior contract to a rate of 
of 
similar to the volun-
tary reduction made by him for 1946, and further, that 
cash dividends equal to $6.00 per share on 1,432,407 
shares, rather than $6.00 per share on 990,179 shares as 
provided under the prior contract, be deducted in arriving 
at the earnings upon which his percentage compensation 
is based. The Board of Directors approved his request and 
has determined that the voluntary reduction in his per-
centage compensation amounted to $706,169.59 for the 
year 1947 and, in accordance with its past practice in 
connection with Mr. Watson's voluntary reductions for 
each of the years 1942 to 1946 inclusive, added this amount 
to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the IBM Retire-
ment Plan. This Fund, after taking into account interest 
income and benefit payments made during 1947, had a 
balance at December 31, 1947 of $2,642,437.75. 
Unfilled Orders 
Although more of the Company's products were pro-
duced in 1947 than in any previous year, unfilled orders 
at the end of the year were higher than at any time in the 
history of the business. Production is being increased to 
meet the continued demand for our products. 
Research and Development 
During the year, our Research and Engineering Labora-
tories continued their work on projects relating to im-
provements in the Company's existing products, and new 
inventions. The IBM Selective Sequence Electronic Cal-
culator was put into operation in the Company's World 
Headquarters building in New York City in December 
1947, and on January 27, 1948 was announced and 
dedicated to the use of science. The IBM Selective 
Sequence Electronic Calculator combines the speed of 


===== PAGE 9 =====
electronic circuits with a memory capacity of 400,000 
digits and the necessary control to utilize this speed and 
capacity in the most complex scientific problems. The 
Calculator will assist scientists in institutions of learning, 
in government, and in industry in solving problems hereto-
fore considered insurmountable because of the time and 
effort required. The Company first extended its develop-
ment beyond the boundaries of commercial business 
machines in 1928, because it was realized that develop-
ments in the scientific computing, measuring and recording 
fields involved principles which can be used in the advance-
ment and improvement of the Company's commercial 
machines. 
In Memoriam 
The Board of Directors solemnly express their deep 
feeling over the death on June 16, 1947 of Mr. Charles A. 
Kirk, at that time Executive Vice-President, Director 
and member of the Executive and Finance Committee. 
The untimely event, which occurred while Mr. Kirk was 
in Europe to attend the International Chamber of Com-
merce Convention and to study conditions abroad as they 
related to the Company's business, was a great shock 
which has brought deep sorrow to the members of the 
Board and the entire IBM organization. Mr. Kirk started 
with IBM in July 1927, and for twenty years served with 
distinction in an unusually comprehensive variety of im-
portant assignments. His record has been and will continue 
to be an inspiration to all members of IBM. 
Annual Meeting of Stockholders 
A Notice, Proxy Statement and form of Proxy for the 
Annual Meeting of Stockholders to be held April 27, 1948 
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1948.

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION: 
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and domestic and Canadian 
subsidiary companies as of December 31, 1947 and the consolidated 
income and earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have 
reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting procedures 
of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed audit of 
the transactions, have examined accounting records of the domestic 
companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we deemed 
appropriate. 
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on 
the accounts of the Canadian subsidiary and the available financial 
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by 
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign 
subsidiaries and branches. Our examination was made in accordance 
with generally accepted auditing standards applicable in the circum-
stances and included all procedures which we considered necessary. 
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and 
related consolidated income and earned surplus account fairly present 
the financial condition of the International Business Machines Cor-
poration and domestic and Canadian subsidiary companies at De-
cember 31, 1947 and the results of operations for the year ended on 
that date, in conformity with generally accepted accounting principles 
applied on a basis consistent with that of the preceding year. 
R. G. RANKIN & CO. 
March 4, 1948
