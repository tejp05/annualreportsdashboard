The net profit for 1942 after taxes and renegotiation 
settlement equalled $8.40 per share on the 990,116 shares 
then outstanding. In 1943, the Company added 49,430 
shares through a stock dividend, making a total of 1,039,546 
shares on which the net profit after taxes for 1943 was $8.85 
per share. 


===== PAGE 6 =====
The net profit for 1943 of $36,974,884.62 before taxes 
compares with $25,019,755.49 for the year 1942 (after 
renegotiation settlement as described in letter to stock-
holders dated February 8, 1944), an increase of $11,955,-
129.13. 
Included in the total taxes of $27,770,000.00 is $22,482,-
000.00 Excess Profits Taxes after deducting post-war 
credits of $2,658,000.00. These credits are carried on the 
Balance Sheet under "Other Assets" pending payment 
by the Government after the war, the total of such credits 
being $3,981,189.02 as of December 31, 1943. 
The net profit of $9,204,884.62 after taxes compares 
with $8,318,941.82 for 1942 (after renegotiation settle-
ment), an increase of $885,942.80. 
The net profit for the year 1943 is after provision for 
voluntary renegotiation of war contracts made with the 
United States Government computed in the same manner 
as was accepted by the Government for the year 1942, 
Cash dividends totaling $6,224,289.00 were paid during 
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of 
$1.50 per share. In addition, a stock dividend of 5 per cent 
(51,897 shares) was declared in November, 1943, and 
issued January 28, 1944, to stockholders of record Jan-
uary 14, 1944. 
In 1941, the year the United States entered the war, 
the Company started making provision from earnings for 
wartime uncertainties and the period of readjustment 
which will follow as a natural result of the war. In 1941, 
$500,000.00 was provided; in 1942, $1,500,000.00; and in 
1943, $4,000,000.00. These provisions, together with the 
$1,000,000.00 provided from Surplus and carried at De-
cember 31,1942 as a reserve for contingencies, comprise at 
December 31, 1943 a Reserve for After-War Adjustments 
and Contingencies of $7,000,000.00. 
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in De-
cember 1943, on the Corporation's holdings of 100,000 
shares of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart Manu-
facturing Company. 
During 1943 there was invested $11,700,921.54 in Rental 
Machines, Plant and Equipment and Land and Buildings. 


===== PAGE 7 =====
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to $2,722,-
326.81 was written off and charged against reserves pro-
vided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
The Corporation is substantially engaged in the manu-
facture of munitions for the United States Government. 
In 1943 the Corporation's plant at Poughkeepsie, New 
York received the Army-Navy "E" for "high achieve-
ment in the production of war materials" and the plant at 
Endicott, New York received the White Star award, 
having received the "E" award in 1942. 
In recognition of the outstanding performance of their 
duties in connection with plant protection, the Army 
Guidon Award has been presented to the guard forces at 
the Company's plants at Endicott, New York, and 
Washington, D. C. 
During 1943 our Research and Engineering Departments 
worked on forty-nine different important projects for the 
U. S. Army, Navy and Air Forces. Satisfactory progress 
also was made in carrying on improvements in the Com-
pany's regular products as well as in new developments 
for the post-war period. 
As of this date, 26 per cent of our men are serving in 
the armed forces of the United States. Of these men in 
service, 23 per cent are commissioned officers. Our women 
are serving in every branch of service open to them. 
A notice, proxy statement and form of proxy for the 
annual meeting of stockholders to be held April 25, 1944, 
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 3,1944. 
By Order of the Board of Directors, 
THOMAS J. WATSON, 
President. 
March 21, 1944

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL 
BUSINESS 
MACHINES 
CORPORATION: 
We have examined the Consolidated Balance Sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian 
Subsidiary Companies as of December 31, 1943 and the Consolidated 
Income and Earned Surplus Account for the year then ended. We 
have reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting pro-
cedures of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed 
audit of the transactions, have examined accounting records of the 
domestic companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we 
deemed appropriate. 
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on 
the accounts of the Canadian Subsidiary and the available 
financial 
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by 
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches located in countries other than enemy con-
trolled and countries where exchange is blocked. Our examination 
was made in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards 
applicable in the circumstances and included all procedures which we 
considered necessary, except that it was impracticable to obtain con-
firmation on all accounts due from the United States Government but 
we satisfied ourselves by other means as to the substantial accuracy 
of such accounts. 
In our opinion, the accompanying Consolidated Balance Sheet and 
related Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account fairly 
present the financial condition of the International Business Ma-
chines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Com-
panies as at December 31, 1943 and the results of operations for the 
year ended on that date, in conformity with generally accepted 
accounting principles applied on a basis consistent with that of the 
preceding year. 
R. G. RANKIN & CO. 
Certified Public Accountants
