T o THE STOCKHOLDERS: 
Your Directors submit herewith Consolidated Income and 
Surplus Account for the year 1933, together with Con-
solidated Balance Sheet as of DecemlDer 31, 1933, with the 
Auditor's certificate attached. 
The Net Income of the year, including Foreign Subsidiaries 
and Branches, before deducting Federal Taxes, but after 
providing for full Depreciation ($2,505,130.94), Development 
and Patent Expenses ($836,180.75), and Interest ($73,918.42) 
was $6,651,424.68, compared with $7,382,757.33 for the year 
1932, being a decrease of $731,332.65. 
After deducting estimated 
Federal Taxes, 
totalling 
$915,000.00, the Net Income was $8.15 per share on 703,345 
shares outstanding December 31,1933, compared with $9.11 
per share for the year 1932, based on the same number of 
shares. 
Foreign profits for 1933 compared with 1932 showed an 
increase of over 15%, after absorbing substantially increased 
foreign taxes. 
Development and patent expenses of $836,180.75 during 
1933 were the highest for any year in our history. 
Net Current Assets at the end of the year amounted to 
$3,623,918.48, compared with $8,299,026.61 at the close of 
1932. The decrease was caused principally by payments 
made for retirement of $1,488,000.00 par value of bonds, 
plus a premium of $74,400.00, or a total of $1,562,400.00; by 
expenditures totalling $1,356,749.41 for land and necessary 
new buildings; the balance of the decrease consisting of 
expenditures for plant machinery, tools and other equipment, 
and new and improved types of rental machines. 
The balance sheet item of $7,063,367.52, shown as "In-

vestments," (to the extent of over 90%) represents the cost 
value of your Company's investment in foreign subsidiaries 
(almost entirely wholly owned) and branches, and such in-
vestments are, in the opinion of the management, carried at 
much less than the present day value. 
The item of "Plant Equipment and Rental Machines" 
shown in the balance sheet at a total of $28,369,644.58, is 
after writing off in 1933 dismantled and obsolete equipment 
totalhng over $3,000,000.00, which has been charged against 
reserves. 
There was carried forward in the Sinking Fund from 1932 
the sum of $490,737.79, and this, together with $1,562,400.00 
paid into the Sinking Fund during 1933, a total of $2,053,-
137.79, provided for the retirement of all remaining out-
standing bonds of an original issue of $7,000,000.00. With 
the exception of the mortgage of $192,500.00, assumed in 
1933 at the time of the merger, this leaves the Company 
with no capital obligations other than common stock. 
In June, 1933, in order to facilitate the operations of your 
Company, all of the domestic subsidiaries were merged with 
the parent company. INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES 
CORPORATION. 
Your Company has co-operated in every way with the 
National Industrial Recovery Act, and a census of our 
employees shows that from July 1, 1933, to December 31, 
1933, our total personnel has been increased an average of 
23.8 percent. The increase in the average hourly rate of 
wages during 1933, due to N.R.A. Code operations, was 
approximately 17.8 percent. 
Your Company has proceeded with its building program, 
and during the year constructed such additional buildings 
at Endicott, New York as were necessary to enable us to 
meet the requirements occasioned by increasing sales. Our 
new engineering laboratory and school buildings put us in 
position to insure our future through the development of 
new devices to meet new needs and the training of men for 
all departments of our business. 


===== PAGE 7 =====
New machines completed during the year were the 
alphabetic electric tabulator, alphabetic printing punch and 
a bank proof machine for sorting, listing and totalling checks. 
In addition many important improvements were made to 
existing types of machines and equipment. These new 
business machines will enable us to broaden our market. 
In order to protect the future of this business more devices 
are in process of development in our engineering and research 
laboratory than at any previous time. 
In 1933 your Company acquired Electromatic Type-
writers, Inc. of Rochester, New York. During 1933 the 
Company also acquired "Radiotype," a device for the trans-
mission of graphic information using the Electromatic type-
writer as a basic machine. We believe that the adaptation of 
these two devices to some of our existing equipment and 
their development in other fields, as well as the sale of the 
machines themselves, will result in satisfactory profits to 
your Company. 
Sales showed a definite upward trend in the latter part of 
1933. December, 1933, was the largest December in the 
Company's history, and January, 1934, was the largest 
January in the Company's history. February, 1934, shows a 
substantial increase over February, 1933. With our new 
machines and increased and well trained sales force we have 
every reason to expect the increase to continue. 
Our volume of unfilled orders is larger than it has been 
for several years. 
By order of the Board of Directors, 
THOMAS J. WATSON, 
President.
