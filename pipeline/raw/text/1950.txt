To the Stockholders: 
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account 
for the year 1950 of the Corporation and subsidiary com-
panies, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the domestic com-
panies as of December 31, 1950, the Auditor's Certificate, 
and a Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past 
Ten Years. 
The results of the year's operations were as follows:

The Net Income for the year of $61,101,308.65 before 
U.S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes compares 
with $53,202,332.22 for 1949, an increase of $7,898,976.43. 
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes for the

Taxes in the amount of $2,100,000.00) compares with

increase of $7,875,000.00. 
The Net Income for the year 
of $33,301,308.65 compares with $33,277,332.22 in 1949,

The Net Income in 1950 equalled $12.05 per share on 
the 2,763,548 shares of capital stock outstanding at the 
end of the year, and compares with $12.04 in 1949 if 
computed on the same number of shares. 
Dividends 
Cash dividends totaling $11,044,169.00 were paid dur-
ing the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends 
of $1.00 per share. 


===== PAGE 6 =====
On October 25, 1949, a 5% stock dividend was declared, 
payable to stockholders of record at the close of business 
on January 5, 1950. 
On January 25, 1950, a total of 
131,541 shares of capital stock was issued in payment of 
this stock dividend, and $13,154,075.00, representing 
$100.00 for each share so issued, was transferred from 
Earned Surplus to Capital Stock account. 
On October 25, 1950, a 5% stock dividend was declared 
payable on January 26, 1951, to stockholders of record 
at the close of business on January 4, 1951. 
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1951, 
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of 
shares, is the one hundred and forty-fourth consecutive 
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company. 
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment 
During the year 1950, the Company's increased volume 
of business and replacement of obsolete equipment re-
quired an investment of $64,855,089.17 in rental machines 
and parts, factory and office equipment, and land and 
buildings. 
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amount-
ing to $21,374,476.87 was written off and charged against 
reserves provided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
Long'Term Indebtedness 
No changes occurred during 1950 in the Corporation's 
long-term indebtedness, which remained at $85,000,000.00 
at December 31, 1950, on which the average rate of 
interest is 2.65%. 
IBM World Trade Corporation 
The growth of IBM's business abroad resulted in the 
formation of the IBM World Trade Corporation, a wholly-
owned but independently operated subsidiary company, 
for the purpose of conducting all IBM business outside 
the United States and its territories and possessions. On 
January 1, 1950, International Business Machines Cor-
poration transferred to the IBM World Trade Corpora-
tion all of its net foreign assets and securities of and 
advances to foreign subsidiaries and branches. 
The net assets of IBM World Trade Corporation and 


===== PAGE 7 =====
its subsidiaries located in Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, 
Germany, Hungary, Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan 
Countries continue to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance 
Sheet pending more settled conditions in these countries. 
During the year 1950 the Corporation adjusted its invest-
ment in these countries by $2,406,545.74, representing 
net income from current operations remaining in these 
countries and other investment adjustments, and, in ac-
cordance with its past practice, added a similar amount 
to the reserve for foreign investment. 
The Corporation 
has not included in its current income account any income 
from these countries not actually received in dollars. 
Foreign investment in "All Other Countries," stated on 
the December 31, 1950, balance sheet at $32,342,476.41, 
is comparable to $26,904,889.33 at December 31, 1949, 
an increase of $5,437,587.08, the result of the current 
year's operations. 
IBM World Trade Corporation's Canadian subsidiary 
has under construction a new factory building at Toronto, 
Canada, which upon completion will add 123,000 square 
feet of floor space to manufacturing facilities at that 
location. 
Employees' Retirement Plan 
As previously reported to stockholders, employees re-
tired after ten or more years of service are provided a 
retirement income under the IBM Retirement Plan, sup-
plemented by the Watson Fund for Supplementing the 
IBM Retirement Plan. 
The Board of Directors revised retirement benefits 
December 1, 1950, increasing the minimum retirement 
income after ten years of continuous service to $75.00 
per month, including Social Security, as compared with 
the previous minimum of $60.00, including Social Security, 
and increasing the maximum retirement income after forty-
five years of continuous service to $240.00 per month in-
cluding Social Security, from the previous maximum of 
$225.00. Retirement income, as heretofore, is based solely 
on length of continuous service, regardless of an employee's 
earnings or position with the Company. The amended plan 
was approved by the Bureau of Internal Revenue. A copy 
of the plan as sent to employees is enclosed.

In December 1950, the Company created an irrevocable 
trust from which will be paid that portion of retirement 
benefits payable from the Watson Fund for Supplementing 
the IBM Retirement Plan. 
Retirement Plan costs totaled $1,771,815.38 during 
1950. 
This amount includes payments 
aggregating 
$395,177.38 to the Trustee and insurance company under 
the IBM Retirement Plan, and payment to the Trustee 
of $1,376,638.00 from the Watson Fund for Supplementing 
the IBM Retirement Plan. 
The Balance Sheet at December 31, 1949 reflected a 
Reserve for Estimated Cost of Employees' Past Service 
Under the IBM Retirement Plan in the amount of 
$3,703,445.50. 
As a result of payments to the Trustee 
and the insurance company since 1945, and the revision 
which occurred in 1950 of the Social Security Act and of 
retirement benefits, this Reserve was no longer required 
and was therefore transferred to Earned Surplus in 
December 1950. 
Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan 
For the year 1950, the Board of Directors added 
$1,175,666.42 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the 
IBM Retirement Plan. 
This amount was determined 
by the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by 
Mr. Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation 
under his present employment contract as compared with 
his prior contract and, in accordance with its past practice, 
as recommended by Mr. Watson and approved by the 
Board of Directors in connection with Mr. Watson's 
voluntary reductions for each year 1942 to 1949, inclusive, 
the above amount was added to the Watson Fund for 
Supplementing the IBM Retirement Plan. 
The Fund, 
after taking into account interest income and benefit 
payments including the $1,376,638.00 payment to the 
Trustee, previously mentioned, had a balance at December 
31, 1950 of $4,383,231.36. 
Unfilled Orders 
Although more of the Company's products were pro-
duced during 1950 than in any year in the history of the 


===== PAGE 9 =====
business, unfilled orders at the end of the year remained 
at a high level. In addition to the demand for our regular 
products, the Company is also committed to a substantial 
amount of defense work under direct contracts with the 
United States Government. 
Research and Development 
The Company's Research and Engineering Laboratories 
continued their work during 1950 on projects relating to 
new inventions, and improvements of our existing prod-
ucts. 
New products added to the IBM regular line in 
1950 included decimal tabulation and partial carriage 
return features for the IBM electric tj^^ewriter; a new 
light-weight electric time stamp; an IBM card verifier 
with increased efficiency and ease of operation which 
verifies alphabetical and numerical punching; a dual feed 
tape carriage for IBM electric accounting machines, 
designed to control the feeding of two different sizes and 
types of continuous forms independently and concur-
rently; and an advanced model card programmed elec-
tronic calculator facilitating solution of a much wider 
range of problems than was heretofore possible. 
In Memoriam 
It is with deep regret that your Board of Directors 
reports the death of Mr. Lamar Hardy, who served with 
distinction as a director of the Corporation from 1948 
until his death on August 18,1950.

Annual Meeting of Stockholders to be held April 24, 1951, 
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1951.

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION: 
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of International 
Business Machines Corporation and domestic subsidiary company as 
of December 31, 1950, and the related consolidated income and 
earned surplus account for the year then ended. 
We have also 
examined the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation, wholly-
owned subsidiary of International Business Machines Corporation, 
for the year ended December 31, 1950. 
Our examination was made 
in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards, and accord-
ingly included such tests of the accounting records and such other 
auditing procedures as we considered necessary in the circumstances. 
We accepted, after review, available reports of independent auditors 
on the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation's foreign subsidia-
ries and branches supplemented by the companies' own statements and 
cabled information. 
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and 
consolidated income and earned surplus account present fairly the 
financial position of the companies at December 31, 1950, and the 
results of their operations for the year then ended, in conformity with 
generally accepted accounting principles applied on a basis consistent 
with that of the preceding year. We further certify to the correctness 
of the financial information presented in the comparative statement 
of operations for the past ten years. 
R. G. RANKIN & CO. 
February 27, 1951.
