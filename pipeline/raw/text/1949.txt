To the Stockholders: 
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account 
for the year 1949 of the Corporation and subsidiary 
companies, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the domestic 
companies as of December 31, 1949, the Auditor's Cer-
tificate, and a Comparative Statement of Operations for 
the Past Ten Years. 
The IBM World Trade Corporation, a wholly-owned 
subsidiary company, is now conducting all IBM's business 
outside the United States and its territories and posses-
sions, Therefore, the assets and liabilities and the results 
of operations of the Canadian company, formerly con-
solidated with those in the United States, are included 
with foreign operations. The assets and liabilities and 
the results of operations in territories and possessions of 
the United States, formerly included with foreign opera-
tions, are included with those in the United States. 
The Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past 
Ten Years has been revised accordingly. 
Net Income 
The net income for the year of $33,277,332.22 compares 
with $28,100,534.82 for 1948, an increase of $5,176,797.40. 
The net income in 1949 equalled $12.64 per share on the 
2,632,007 shares of capital stock outstanding at the end of 
the period, and compares with $10.68 in 1948, if computed 
on the same number of shares. 
Dividends 
Cash dividends totaling $10,518,821.00 were paid during 
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of 
$1.00 per share. 
On October 26,1948, a 5% stock dividend was declared, 
payable to stockholders of record at the close of business 
on January 7, 1949. On January 28, 1949, a total of 
125,295 shares of capital stock was issued in payment of 
this stock dividend, and $12,529,561.25, representing 


===== PAGE 6 =====
$100.00 for each share so issued, was transferred from 
Earned Surplus to Capital Stock account. 
On October 25, 1949, a 5% stock dividend was declared 
payable on January 25, 1950, or as soon thereafter as 
practicable, to stockholders of record at the close of 
business on January 5, 1950. 
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1950, 
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of 
shares, is the one hundred and fortieth consecutive 
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company. 
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment 
During the year 1949, the Company's increased volume 
of business and replacement of obsolete equipment re-
quired an investment of $56,472,351.27 in rental machines 
and parts, factory and office equipment, and land and 
buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting 
to $15,562,070.48 was written off and charged against 
reserves provided out of prior and current years' earnings. 
Long'Term Indebtedness 
No changes occurred during 1949 in the Corporation's 
long-term indebtedness, which remains at $85,000,000.00 
at December 31, 1949, on which the average rate of 
interest is 2.65%. 
Investment Outside the United States 
The net assets of the Corporation and its subsidiaries 
located in Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, 
Hungary, Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan Countries 
continue to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet, 
pending more settled conditions in these countries. During 
the year 1949 the Corporation adjusted its investment in 
these countries by $3,239,714.66, representing net income 
from current operations remaining in these countries and 
other investment adjustments, and added a similar amount 
to the reserve for foreign investment. The Corporation 
has not included in its current income account any income 
from these countries not actually received in dollars. 
Foreign investment in "All Other Countries," stated 
on the December 31,1949, balance sheet at $26,904,889.33, 


===== PAGE 7 =====
includes the Corporation's investment in its Canadian 
subsidiary company, but is exclusive of its investment in 
the territories and possessions of the United States now 
consolidated with assets in the United States, as previously 
explained. The investment of $26,904,889.33 is comparable 
to $23,295,517.85 at December 31,1948, on a similar basis, 
or an increase of $3,609,371.48, the result of the current 
year's operations. 
By agreement dated October 14, 1949, the Corporation 
and The British Tabulating Machine Company, Limited, 
cancelled the contract relationship which had been in 
existence since March 31, 1908. As the result of such 
cancellation IBM is now enabled to extend its operations 
throughout the entire British Commonwealth and Empire, 
in competition with the British Company, and the latter 
is relieved from its obligation to continue the payment of 
patent royalties to the Corporation. 
Employees' Retirement Plan 
Cost of the IBM Retirement Plan for 1949 totaled 
$2,114,685.28. This amount includes $663,094.10, the fifth 
payment to the Endicott Trust Company as Trustee of 
the IBM Retirement Plan, of which amount $500,000.00 
was prepaid in October 1948 and charged in that year to 
the previously established Reserve for Estimated Cost of 
Employees' Past Service under the Plan, and the balance 
of $163,094.10, paid during 1949, was charged to this 
reserve in 1949. The current year's premium of $1,451,-
591.18, paid under the IBM Retirement Plan to The 
Equitable Life Assurance Society of the United States, 
was charged against income. 
Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan 
For the year 1949, the Board of Directors added 
$1,012,471.68 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the 
IBM Retirement Plan. This amount was determined by 
the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by Mr. 
Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation under 
his present employment contract as compared with his 
prior contract and, in accordance with its past practice, 
as recommended by Mr. Watson and approved by the 


===== PAGE 8 =====
Board of Directors in connection with Mr. Watson's 
voluntary reductions for each year 1942 to 1948, inclusive, 
the above amount was added to the Watson Fund for 
Supplementing the IBM Retirement Plan. The Fund, 
after taking into account interest income and benefit 
payments made during 1949, had a balance at December 
31, 1949, of $4,538,650.37. 
Unfilled Orders 
Although more of the Company's products were pro-
duced during 1949 than in any year in the history of the 
business, unfilled orders at the end of the year remained 
at a high level, and production has been increased to meet 
the continued demand for our products. 
Research and Development 
During the year, our Research and Engineering Labora-
tories continued their work on projects relating to new 
inventions and improvements of our existing products. 
New products added to the IBM regular line in 1949 
include an electric accounting machine which lists alpha-
betical and numerical information at 150 lines per minute, 
an increase of fifty per cent over existing models, and 
which has considerably ^eater capacity and flexibility; 
a new card punch with increased efficiency and ease of 
operation; an alphabetical collator which processes cards 
punched with alphabetical information, codes and other 
designations; the Cardatype, a device which makes pos-
sible the automatic operation of the IBM electric type-
writer by means of IBM cards; an electronic statistical 
machine which combines in one unit, for the first time, the 
functions of sorting, counting, accumulating, balancing, 
editing, and printing of information; a card-programmed 
electronic calculator embodying developments from the 
construction of the IBM Selective Sequence Electronic 
Calculator, the card-programmed electronic calculator rep-
resenting IBM's third commercial application of electron-
ics for calculating, the first such commercial calculator being 
introduced by IBM in 1946; a new proof machine for sorting 
and listing, totaling and endorsing bank checks, sales slips 
and similar documents; and a time recorder of increased 


===== PAGE 9 =====
flexibility, which can be used as a job recorder, as an 
attendance time recorder, or as a combination of the two. 
IBM Officers 
At the September 19, 1949, meeting of the Board of 
Directors, Mr. Thomas J. Watson, President, and head of 
the Corporation since 1914, was elected Chairman of the 
Board and Chairman of the Executive and Finance Com-
mittee. Mr. Watson continues to serve as chief executive 
officer of the Corporation. Mr. John G. Phillips, Executive 
Vice President, and member of the Board and of the 
Executive and Finance Committee, was elected President 
of the Corporation. Mr. Phillips has been an officer of the 
Corporation since 1923. Mr. Thomas J. Watson, Jr., Vice 
President and director of the Corporation since 1946, was 
elected Executive Vice President. 
IBM World Trade Corporation 
The growth of IBM's business abroad resulted in the 
formation of the IBM W^orld Trade Corporation, a wholly-
owned but independently operated subsidiary company, 
for the purpose of conducting all IBM business outside 
the United States and its territories and possessions. 
On January 1, 1950, International Business Machines 
Corporation transferred to the IBM World Trade Corpo-
ration all of its net foreign assets and securities of and 
advances to foreign subsidiaries and branches. 
Mr. Thomas J. Watson, in addition to his duties in 
International Business Machines Corporation, is serving 
as Chairman of the Board, the chief executive officer of 
IBM World Trade Corporation. 
Annual Meeting of Stockholders 
A Notice, Proxy Statement and form of Proxy for the 
Annual Meeting of Stockholders to be held April 25,1950, 
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1950. 
By Order of the Board of Directors, 
THOMAS J. WATSON, 
Chairman of the Board.

To the Stockholders of the 
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION: 
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and domestic subsidiary 
companies as of December 31, 1949, and the related consoHdated 
income and earned surplus account for the year then ended. Our 
examination was made in accordance with generally accepted audit-
ing standards, and accordingly included such tests of the accounting 
records and such other auditing procedures as we considered necessary 
in the circumstances. 
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on 
the accounts of the non-consolidated Canadian subsidiary and the 
available financial statements, mainly the company's own statements 
supplemented by cabled information, on the accounts of the other 
non-consolidated foreign subsidiaries and branches. 
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and 
consolidated income and earned surplus account present fairly the 
financial position of the companies at December 31, 1949, and the 
results of their operations for the year then ended, in conformity 
with generally accepted accounting principles applied on a basis 
consistent with that of the preceding year. We further certify to 
the correctness of the financial information presented in the com-
parative statement of operations for the past ten years. 
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
