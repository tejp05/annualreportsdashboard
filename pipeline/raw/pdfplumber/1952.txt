To the Stockholders:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1952 of the Corporation and subsidiary com-
panies, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the domestic com-
panies as of December 31, 1952, the Auditor's Certificate,
and a Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past
Ten Years.
The results of the year's operations were as follows:

The Net Income for the year of $78,474,541.37 before
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes compares
with $77,292,089.68 for 1951, an increase of $1,182,451.69.
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes for the

The Net Income in 1952 equalled $9.81 per share on the

the year, and compares with $9.16 in 1951, if computed on
the same number of shares.

Dividends
Cash dividends totaling $12,172,832.00 were paid dur-
ing the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of
$1.00 per share.
On November 13, 1951, a 5% stock dividend was de-
clared, payable to stockholders of record at the close of
business on January 4, 1952. On January 28, 1952, a total
of 144,989 shares of capital stock was issued in payment of
this stock dividend, and $21,748,320.00, representing
$150.00 for each share so issued, was transferred from
Earned Surplus to Capital Stock account.
On October 28, 1952, a 5% stock dividend was declared
payable on January 29, 1953 to stockholders of record at
the close of business on January 5, 1953.
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1953,
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of
shares, is the one hundred and fifty-second consecutive
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company.
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment
During the year 1952, the Corporation's increased vol-
ume of business and replacement of obsolete equipment
required an investment of $89,282,023.17 in rental ma-
chines and parts, factory and office equipment, and land
and buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amount-
ing to $26,005,384.93 was written off and charged against
reserves provided out of prior and current years' earnings.
At December 31, 1952, the balances of the Corporation's
reserves for depreciation and amortization of rental ma-
chines and parts, factory and office equipment, and land
and buildings aggregated $180,519,290.87.
On June 14, 1952, the Corporation's new factory addi-
tion at Poughkeepsie, New York, was dedicated, providing
an additional 632,000 square feet of floor space of manu-

===== PAGE 7 =====
facturing facilities at that location. These additional facili-
ties, built under a necessity certificate granted by the
United States Government, are required for production
under defense contracts.
As previously announced, construction of a research
laboratory will be started at Poughkeepsie, New York, in
the near future, to be completed before the end of 1954.
This will add 179,000 square feet of floor space to the
Corporation's existing laboratory facilities at that location.
Construction of a new plant will also be started in 1953
on the Corporation's recently acquired property at Green-
castle, Indiana. This plant, comprising 180,000 square feet
of floor space, will be devoted to the manufacture of IBM
cards used in connection with the Company's electric and
electronic accounting machines.
Long-Term Indebtedness
On February 28, 1952 the Corporation took down
$40,000,000.00 of the $115,000,000.00 included in the Cor-
poration's agreement of January 22, 1952 with The Pru-
dential Insurance Company of America. As previously
reported to stockholders, under this agreement the Cor-
poration will borrow from The Prudential Insurance Com-
pany a total of $115,000,000.00 against the Corporation's
notes, to mature January 1, 2052. The remaining
$75,000,000.00 of notes will be issued in various amounts
from time to time, not later than December 31, 1954.
There are no sinking fund requirements, but optional pre-
payments may be made without premium from available
funds arising out of operations or from the sale of stock.
After January 1, 1960, at the option of either the Corpora-
tion or Prudential, the notes may be converted as a whole
but not in part into 25-year 33^% notes, with provision
for equal annual prepayments, without premium, com-

===== PAGE 8 =====
mencing with the sixth year after the date of such conver-
sion, to effect complete retirement of the notes by their
maturity. The notes sold in 1952, together with the
Corporation's other long-term indebtedness, brought the
total long-term indebtedness to $175,000,000,00 at Decem-
ber 31,1952, on which the average rate of interest is 3.14%.
Unfilled Orders
Although more of the Corporation's products were pro-
duced in 1952 than in any year in the history of the busi-
ness, unfilled orders at the end of the year were higher
than at the end of any previous year. In addition to the
continued demand for the Corporation's regular products,
the Corporation is engaged in a substantial amount of
defense work under direct contracts with the United
States Government.
Employees' Retirement Plan
Retirement Plan payments to the Trustee totaled
$3,014,811.92 during 1952. This amount includes payments
aggregating $1,466,868.92 under the IBM Retirement
Plan, and $1,547,943.00 from the Watson Fund for Sup-
plementing the IBM Retirement Plan.
Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan

$1,711,740.66 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the
IBM Retirement Plan. This amount was determined by
the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by Mr.
Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation under
his present employment contract. In accordance with its
past practice, as recommended by Mr. Watson and ap-
proved by the Board of Directors in connection with Mr.
Watson's voluntary reductions for each year 1942 to 1951

United States Government, Department
of Justice Complaint
For the information of new stockholders, on January 21,
1952, the Department of Justice filed a complaint against
IBM alleging that it is a monopoly. We deny the charge.
The fact is that IBM is one of many business machines
companies in a highly competitive field. Our machines are
used to perform a small percentage of the accounting and
record keeping work of American business and govern-
ment. There are numerous other machines on the market
which are advertised, sold, and used to do the same work
that IBM machines are used to do. To allege that IBM is a
monopoly in view of its position in this competitive field
is to disregard the facts.
IBM has adhered to the highest standards of conform-
ance to all laws and business ethics. We have never em-
ployed any measures to eliminate or restrain competition.
We reject as unfounded any construction of our business
conduct as in violation of the anti-trust laws, and intend
to vindicate our position in the courts.
Research and Development
Our research and engineering laboratories are continu-
ing their work on a number of important defense projects
for the United States Government and on new and im-
proved commercial products.
In 1952 the Corporation completed the first of a series
of IBM Electronic Data Processing Machines. This equip-

===== PAGE 10 =====
ment represents IBM's fourth commercial application of
electronics for calculating, the first such commercial cal-
culator being introduced by IBM in 1946. The new equip-
ment combines for the first time in one machine the great
memory capacity and flexibility of cathode-ray tubes,
magnetic drums, and magnetic tapes, as well as employing
the use of punched cards. These components, together with
other features, enable the computer to solve with high
speed and efficiency a wide variety of intricate problems
of the type that can be reduced to basic arithmetic steps.
IBM World Trade Corporation
The net assets of IBM World Trade Corporation
(wholly-owned subsidiary) and its subsidiaries located in
Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, Hungary,
Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan Countries continue
to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet pending more
settled conditions in these countries. During the year
1952, the Corporation adjusted its investment in these
countries by $7,870,497.92, of which amount $4,380,697.28
represents net income from current operations remaining
in these countries and reinvested in the business, and
$3,489,800.64 an adjustment of recovery values in the
Western Zones of Germany. In accordance with past prac-
tice, the aggregate amount was added to the reserve for
foreign investment. The Corporation has not included in
its current income account any income from the above
countries not actually received in dollars.
Foreign investment in "All Other Countries," stated on
the December 31, 1952 balance sheet at $39,023,542.20,
is comparable to $36,193,354.98 at December 31, 1951, an
increase of $2,830,187.22, the result of the current year's
operations.

The Canadian company is constructing an addition to

pletion will add 200,000 square feet of floor space to

In Memoriam
It is with deep regret that your Directors report the

as directors of the Corporation, Mr. Christopher D.
Smithers on May 27, 1952, and Mr. Augustine L. Humes
on September 25, 1952. Mr. Smithers served as a director
from 1913 until his death, and Mr. Humes since 1940.

INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of International
Business Machines Corporation and domestic subsidiary company as
of December 31, 1952, and the related consolidated income and
earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have also
examined the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation, wholly-
owned subsidiary of International Business Machines Corporation,
for the year ended December 31, 1952. Our examination was made
in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards, and accord-
ingly included such tests of the accounting records and such other
auditing procedures as we considered necessary in the circumstances.
We accepted, after review, available reports of independent auditors
on the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation's foreign subsidia-
ries and branches supplemented by the companies' own statements and
cabled information.
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and
consolidated income and earned surplus account present fairly the
financial position of the companies at December 31, 1952, and the
results of their operations for the year then ended, in conformity with
generally accepted accounting principles applied on a basis consistent
with that of the preceding year. We further certify to the correctness
of the financial information presented in the comparative statement
of operations for the past ten years.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
