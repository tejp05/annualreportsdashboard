To the stockholders:
Looked at in a number of ways—record income
and earnings, significant new products and
technology—1972 was a landmark year for IBM.
But I could not make my initial report to
stockholders as Chairman of the Board without
first expressing gratitude on behalf of the entire
Company to T. Vincent Learson, who retired
as IBM Board Chairman on December 31,1972.
His vigorous leadership and good judgment
guided the Company through many significant
decisions that led to the success of the past year.
Over a truly distinguished 37-year career
with IBM—17 of those years in top corporate
positions-Vin Learson became known not only
as an executive of great energy and incisive-
ness, but also as a man who continuously chal-
lenged others to give their best efforts.
Although he has retired as an active em-
ployee, we are all pleased that he will continue
to contribute his knowledge and talents to
the Company as a member of the Board and its
Executive and Finance Committees.
The Company is indeed fortunate that
Thomas J. Watson, Jr., continues as a member
of the Corporate Office. In addition, we have
been joined by Gilbert E. Jones, IBM Senior

of IBM World Trade Corporation, providing

World's Fastest Switch?
Right: This experimental electronic compo-
nent may lead to the computer technology that
will be needed tomorrow. It is a Josephson
junction, which can be as small as one twenty-
five-thousand th-of-an-inch across—so small
that a powerful microscope is needed to see it.
And so fast that it can switch an electric voltage
on or off in less than 10 trillionths of a sec-
ond. It is being studied by IBM research scientists
in Switzerland and the United States.

Reserved...
The Swiss Federal Railways uses an IBM
computer and over 70 terminals to confirm
reservations quickly, and to assure travel-
ers of quality service by alerting train crews in
advance to exact car occupancy. Requests
for reservations are read by the terminals and
checked and confirmed by the computer.
Quick Claims Service...
Policyholders of an insurance company in the
Midwest get immediate answers to questions
about their insurance and prompt claim settle-
ments. Through IBM terminals, the company's
claims representatives can quickly retrieve data
on any policy from an IBM computer and give
policyholders immediate response to their
inquiries. The computer system has cut about
a week off the average time it takes to settle
a claim.

Deliverymen of a dairy in Pennsylvania
now write out orders for dairy products from
137,000 homes and 5,000 grocery stores so that

be delivered the next day. They also use the com-
