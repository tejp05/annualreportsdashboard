1968 was a particularly significant and
eventful year for the IBM Company. It was
a year in which many new trends in the
growth of the data processing industry be-
came apparent, as well as a year of con-
tinued strong demand for the Company's
established products and services through-
out the world.
J968 IBM Financial Results
Consolidated gross income from world-
wide operations amounted to 16,888,549,209,
an increase of 11,543,258,216 over 1967.
Earnings were $871,497,991, a $219,998,43 3
increase over the prior year.
Thomas J. Watson, Jr., IBM Chairman Earnings per share were $7.71 based on
the 112,968,369 shares outstanding at the
end of the year. This compares with earn-
ings of $5.81 per share on the 112,228,558
shares outstanding on December 31,1967,
adjusted for the 100% stock split effected
May 9,1968.
Operations outside the United States,
included in consolidated results, showed
gross income of $2,040,184,359, an increase
of $415,193,617 over 1967. This is the
first year IBM World Trade Corporation's
gross income exceeded the $2 billion
mark. Earnings from these operations were
$270,546,255, an increase of $61,185,795
over the year before.

and earnings showed abnormally high rates
of growth, primarily because of a major in-
crease in the level of outright sales of data
processing equipment. Independent com-
puter leasing companies' purchases of IBM's
System/360 accounted for most of this un-
usually high level of outright sales. Although

Significant 1968 achievements in the area
of computer manufacturing fell primarily
into three categories: additional build-
ing, which has increased production and
efficiency; first customer shipments of new
IBM products; and technological advances
to improve quality and reduce costs.
During the year, the Company com-
pleted or began construction of major ex-
pansions at plants in Boulder, Colorado;
Rochester, Minnesota; Burlington, Vermont;
San Jose, California; and Poughkeepsie
and East Fishkill, New York. In Boca Raton,
Florida, where manufacturing is being
done in temporary facilities, ground was
broken for a new plant. The Company
also announced that a new Components Di-
vision engineering and manufacturing
facility for advanced components and circuit
packages will be established at Manassas,
Virginia, with an initial construction pro-
gram of 250,000 square feet.
IBM's newest manufacturing plant, in
the Bedford-Stuyvesant community of
Brooklyn, New York, began operations dur-
ing 1968. This new facility not only pro-
vides for growing manufacturing needs, but
also allows IBM to address the needs of
the community by providing jobs in an area
where the needs have been great and the
opportunities few. Approximately 130 peo-
ple, hired from the community, are now
manufacturing cables for the Company's
standard computer line. The growth to
300 locally hired people by the end of
1969 will involve the introduction of addi-
tional computer-related products.
