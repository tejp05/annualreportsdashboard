An important step was made by IBM
research scientists in the area of integrated
circuits. A computer was applied to de-
signing and producing the extremely com-
plex ""masks" used in the photographic
fabrication process by which these circuits
are made. From the designer's instructions,
the computer determines the patterns re-
quired on each of many masks and then con-
trols a device which ""draws" the desired
patterns on a photographic plate from
which the actual masks are made. The tech-
nique makes it possible to fabricate and
test new chip circuit designs many times
faster than previously possible.
Company scientists solved some complex
technological problems to produce the first
reported experimental monolithic integrated
circuits made of the element germanium.
Until now, such circuits have been made of
silicon, but since electrons move approxi-
mately three times as fast in germanium as in
silicon, germanium offers ultimately higher
speed in electronic devices.
Advances continue to be made in the area
of lasers, devices which emit intense beams
of coherent light. These developments may
someday be applicable to data storage de-
vices, printing, and optical radars.
A new method of energizing gas lasers
makes it possible to achieve a much wider
bore in the laser tube. This is important
for such applications as a scanning laser in
which the light beam is moved in differ-
ent directions. A way of inducing lasing in a
wide range of dyes was developed, which
provides an inexpensive laser medium that
can emit light in any desired color of the
spectrum.
In related research, an IBM scientist de-
veloped a method of accurately measuring
the duration of extremely short pulses of
laser light or other pulses that last only a
fraction of a trillionth of a second. These are
the most minute time measurements ever
made outside the realm of nuclear physics.

IBM was recently granted a patent stem-
ming from research into the superplasticity
of metals. Certain alloys take on a plastic-
like consistency as they are being worked.
While in this state, the metal can be formed
by many of the same techniques that are
used with plastic. After further processing,
the metal retains its final shape for use.
This represents a metal-working method of
great flexibility and far-ranging utility.

Retirement Plans:
The Company and its domestic subsidiaries
have had for many years a trusteed, non-
contributory retirement plan covering
substantially all their employees. Certain
subsidiaries outside the United States
have retirement plans under which funds
are deposited with trustees, reserves are
provided, or annuities are purchased under
group contracts. The cost of all plans
totaled approximately $72 million for the
year 1967.
