To THE STOCKHOLDERS:
Your directors submit for the information of Stockholders
Consolidated Income and Surplus Account for the year 1936,
together with Consolidated Balance Sheet as at December 31,
1936, with the Auditors' Certificate attached.
Net Income for the year 1936, including Foreign Sub-
sidiaries and Branches, before deducting Federal Taxes, but
after providing for full Depreciation ($3,512,944.85), De-
velopment and Patent Expense ($860,954,71) and Interest
($233,116.61) was $9,127,956.41, compared with $8,127,530.69
for the year 1935, being an increase of $1,000,425,72.
After deducting estimated Federal Taxes of $1,575,000.00
(including $175,000.00 Surtax on Undistributed Profits), the
Net Income for the year was $7,552,956.41, equal to $10.22
per share on 738,934 shares outstanding December 31, 1936,
compared with $7,090,530.69, equal to $9.59 per share, for
the year 1935, based on an equal number of shares, being
an increase of 63 cents per share. The Net Income for 1935,
based on 717,412 shares outstanding at the end of that
year, was $9.88 per share.
The above Income includes a cash dividend of $150,000.00
received in December 1936 on the company's holding of
100,000 shares of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart
Manufacturing Company.
Pursuant to approval of the stockholders at a special
meeting held July 20, 1936, certain foreign assets of the

===== PAGE 6 =====
Dayton Scale Division were transferred to the Hobart
Manufacturing Company which had previously acquired the
domestic business of the Dayton Scale Division, and the
remainder of such assets are in course of transfer.
Net Current Assets at the end of the year were $5,310,-
991.77, compared with $2,665,676.59 at the end of 1935.
Expenditures totalling $9,202,827.43 were made during
the year for rental machines, plant and equipment and land
and buildings, (including new office building). A total of
$4,953,617.61 of dismantled and obsolete equipment was
written off and charged against reserves.
In June, 1936, the Company effected the sale to the
Prudential Insurance Company of America of $10,000,000.00
Ten-Year 3% Sinking Fund Debentures at par. These
Debentures are redeemable at par through a Sinking Fund
commencing June 15, 1941, or earlier at the Corporation's
option, of $800,000.00 each year, or at 101 out of funds
otherwise available from profits or in connection with re-
financing for additional capital; otherwise the Debentures
are redeemable at a premium of 5% on or prior to June 15,
1937, such premium diminishing one-half of 1% annually
thereafter. The proceeds of the sale of these Debentures
effected the retirement of the Company's outstanding bank
loans of $5,900,000.00, and the balance was used for additions
to working capital, and to acquire an office building at 590
Madison Avenue, New York City, in order to accommodate
satisfactorily in one building the executive, sales, service and
clerical forces of the New York offices.
Following the practice adopted in 1935 of setting aside
a reserve in the full amount of the blocked net profits of
foreign subsidiaries, the Company has this year set aside

===== PAGE 7 =====
the amount of $692,822.23, the full amount of such blocked
net profits for the year.
In order to provide houses for our factory employees at
Endicott, New York, there has been formed the Endicott
Highlands Development Corporation, all the stock of which
is owned by your Company. Lands to the estimated value
of $66,000.00, owned by your Company at Endicott, were
transferred to this corporation, and houses are being con-
structed and sold to factory employees. All costs of house
construction, exclusive of the land, are being financed by the
purchasers of the houses through the Federal Housing Admin-
istration, and it is anticipated that the full value of the land
will in due course be returned to your Company.
At the Annual Meeting of the Stockholders of the Com-
pany, held April 7,1936, the authorized Capital Stock of the
Company was increased from 750,000 shares to 1,000,000
shares, all without nominal or par value.
Regular quarterly cash dividends of $1.50 per share were
paid during the year, and also, on account of the Federal
Income Tax Law of 1936, the quarterly dividend of $1.50
per share ordinarily payable on January 10, 1937 was paid
on December 21, 1936. In addition, a stock dividend of 5%
was declared in November 1936, payable April 1, 1937 to
stockholders of record March 15, 1937.
By order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
PRESIDENT.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have made an examination of the accounts and records of the
International Business Machines Corporation and its Domestic Subsidi-
aries relating to the consolidated balance sheet as at December 31, 1936
and reviewed the Consolidated Income and Surplus Account for the year
ended on that date, but we have not made a detailed audit of all trans-
actions, In that connection, we accepted after review an independent
Auditor's Report on the accounts of the Canadian Corporation and the
Company's own financial statements on the accounts of the other
Foreign Subsidiaries and Branches. The Company's accounts have been
consistently maintained in accordance with accepted accounting prin-
ciples and all information and explanations desired were furnished.
The accompanying consolidated balance sheet, based upon such
examination, fairly presents, in our opinion, the financial condition of
the International Business Machines Corporation and its Domestic
and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as at December 31, 1936, and the
related consolidated income and surplus account the results of opera-
tions for the year ended on that date.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
Certified Public Accountants
February 23, 1937
