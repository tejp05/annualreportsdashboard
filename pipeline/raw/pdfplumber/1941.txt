To THE STOCKHOLDERS:
Your directors submit for the information of Stockhold-
ers Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account for
the year 1941, together with Consolidated Balance Sheet of
the domestic companies and the Canadian subsidiary com-
pany as of December 31, 1941, with the Auditor's Certifi-
cate attached.
Net Profit for the year 1941, including foreign subsidi-
aries and branches, but before deducting estimated United
States Federal and Canadian Income and Excess Profits
Taxes and special reserve, was $19,544,633.14, compared
with $13,097,013.10 for the year 1940, being an increase of
$6,447,620.04.
After deducting estimated United States Federal and
Canadian Income and Excess Profits Taxes of $9,200,000.00,
including United States Excess Profits Tax of $4,070,000.00
and also after deducting a special reserve for after-war
adjustments of $500,000.00, the Net Profit for the year
1941 was $9,844,633.14, compared with $9,431,013.10 for
the year 1940, being an increase of $413,620.04. The Net
Profit for 1941 after taxes equals $10.44 per share on the
943,028 shares outstanding December 31, 1941, which
shares include 44,850 shares issued as a stock dividend on
January 30, 1941.

===== PAGE 6 =====
The Investment in and Advances to Subsidiaries and
Branches in enemy-controlled and other blocked exchange
countries amounting to $14,552,772.47 is more than offset
by reserves amounting to $14,677,934.58, of which $6,677,-
934.58 is represented by specific reserves and $8,000,000.00
by the Reserve for Contingencies. The latter reserve was
increased $2,700,000.00 during the year.
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in Decem-
ber 1941 on the Company's holdings of 100,000 shares of
the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart Manufacturing
Company.
There was invested during 1941 $18,623,695.86 in rental
machines, plant and equipment and land and buildings.
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to $3,240,-
760.31 was written off and charged against reserves pro-
vided out of prior and current years' earnings.
In August, 1941 the Company completed the construc-
tion of an additional building to its plant at Endicott,
New York, together with the necessary machinery and
equipment, which has since been in operation.
In July, 1941 the Company sold and transferred certain
of its assets to the National Postal Meter Company, Inc.,
in exchange for 13,832 shares of the latter Company's
"Class A" Capital Stock, and in addition purchased 4,000
shares of Preferred Stock and 4,000 shares of Common
Stock of National Postal Meter Company, Inc., for
$200,000.00.

===== PAGE 7 =====
In October, 1941 the Corporation effected the sale to the
Prudential Insurance Company of America of $17,000,-
000.00 Ten-Year 2^% Sinking Fund Debentures at par.
The Debentures are redeemable through a Sinking Fund
at the rate of $1,500,000.00 annually commencing October
1, 1946, or earlier at the Corporation's option. The pro-
ceeds of the sale of these Debentures was used to retire
the outstanding balance of the Corporation's Ten-Year
3% Sinking Fund Debentures due June 15, 1946, and its
Ten-Year 33^% Sinking Fund Debentures due December
15, 1947; and to provide additional working capital.
The Company is committed to a large amount of war
work under direct contracts with the United States Gov-
ernment and through the Munitions Manufacturing Cor-
poration in which the Company has as of today a 94.3%
interest.
Four quarterly cash dividends of $1.50 per share were
made during the year. In addition, a stock dividend of 5%
(47,088 Shares) was declared in November, 1941 and issued
January 28, 1942 to stockholders of record January 14,
1942.
By order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the Consolidated Balance Sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian
Subsidiary Companies as of December 31, 1941, and the Consolidated
Income and Earned Surplus Account for the fiscal year then ended. In
connection therewith, we have reviewed the system of internal control
and the accounting procedures of the domestic companies and, with-
out making a detailed audit of the transactions, have examined or
tested accounting records of the domestic companies and other sup-
porting evidence, by methods and to the extent we deemed appropriate.
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on
the accounts of the Canadian Subsidiary and the available financial
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by
cable information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches located in countries which are not enemy con-
trolled and in other countries not subject to exchange restrictions.
Inasmuch as recent statements were not available, the investments in
and advances to foreign subsidiaries and branches located in enemy
controlled countries and other countries subject to exchange restric-
tions are stated, substantially, at the December 31, 1940 values, after
giving effect to subsequently reported inter-company transactions.
In our opinion, the accompanying Consolidated Balance Sheet and
related Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account present
fairly the financial condition of the International Business Machines
Corporation and Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as at
December 31, 1941 and the results of operations for the year ended
on that date, in conformity with generally accepted accounting prin-
ciples applied on a basis consistent with that of the preceding year.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
Certified Public Accountants
