To the Stockholders:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1951 of the Corporation and subsidiary com-
panies, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the domestic com-
panies as of December 31, 1951, the Auditor's Certificate,
and a Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past
Ten Years.
The results of the year's operations were as follows:
Net Income

Net Income for the year $27,892,089.68
The Net Income for the year of $77,292,089.68 before
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes compares
with $61,101,308.65 for 1950, an increase of $16,190,781.03.
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes for the
year 1951 of $49,400,000.00 (which includes Excess Profits
Taxes in the amount of $8,950,000.00) compares with
$27,800,000.00 for 1950, an increase of $21,600,000.00. The
Net Income for the year of $27,892,089.68 compares with
$33,301,308.65 in 1950, a decrease of $5,409,218.97.
The Net Income in 1951 equalled $9.61 per share on the
2,901,652 shares of capital stock outstanding at the end of
the year, and compares with $11.48 in 1950, if computed
on the same number of shares.

Dividends
Cash dividends totaling $11,577,189.00 were paid dur-
ing the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of
$1.00 per share.
On October 25,1950, a 5% stock dividend was declared,
payable to stockholders of record at the close of business
on January 4,1951. On January 26,1951, a total of 138,104
shares of capital stock was issued in payment of this stock
dividend, and $17,262,981.25, representing $125.00 for
each share so issued, was transferred from Earned Surplus
to Capital Stock account.
On November 13, 1951, a 5% stock dividend was de-
clared payable on January 28, 1952, to stockholders of
record at the close of business on January 4, 1952.
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1952,
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of
shares, is the one hundred and forty-eighth consecutive
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company.
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment
During the year 1951, the Company's increased volume
of business and replacement of obsolete equipment re-
quired an investment of $88,509,543.82 in rental machines
and parts, factory and office equipment, and land and
buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting
to $15,266,530.75 was written off and charged against
reserves provided out of prior and current years' earnings.
At December 31, 1951, the balances of the Company's
reserves for depreciation and amortization of rental ma-
chines and parts, factory and office equipment, and land
and buildings aggregated $150,596,074.02.
In March 1951, the Corporation started construction of
two additional three-story structures to its plant at Pough-
keepsie. New York, which will add 632,000 square feet of

===== PAGE 7 =====
floor space, approximately doubling the size of this plant.
These additional facilities, built under a necessity certifi-
cate granted by the United States Government, are re-
quired for production under defense contracts.
Long-Term Indebtedness
Under agreement dated May 28, 1951 the Corporation
issued and sold on October 18, 1951 and November 5,
1951, its 33^% promissory notes in the principal amounts
of $20,000,000.00 and $30,000,000.00, respectively, ma-
turing December 1, 1971, to The Prudential Insurance
Company of America, at 100% of such principal amounts.
These notes, together with the Corporation's other long-
term indebtedness, brought the total long-term indebted-
ness to $135,000,000.00 at December 31, 1951, on which
the average rate of interest is 2.96%.
On January 22, 1952, the Corporation entered into an
agreement with The Prudential Insurance Company to
borrow $115,000,000.00 against the Corporation's 100-
year 3^% notes, to mature January 1, 2052. The notes
will be issued in various amounts from time to time, not
later than December 31, 1954. There are no sinking fund
requirements, but optional prepayments may be made
without premium from available funds arising out of
operations or from the sale of stock. After January 1,1960,
at the option of either the Corporation or Prudential, the
notes may be converted as a whole but not in part into
twenty-five year 3^% notes, with provision for equal
annual prepayments, without premium, commencing with
the sixth year after the date of such conversion, to effect
complete retirement of the notes by their maturity.
As announced at the time of entering into the foregoing
agreements, the proceeds will be used for the consti'uction
of the plant addition at Poughkeepsie, New York; addi-
tional manufacturing equipment required for production

under Defense Program contracts at the Poughkeepsie
and Endicott, New York, plants; to provide additional
working capital required by the continued growth of the
business and for other corporate purposes.
Unfilled Orders
Although more of the Company's products were pro-
duced during 1951 than in any year in the history of the
business, unfilled orders at the end of the year remained
at a high level. In addition to the continued demand for
the Company's regular products, the Company is engaged
in a substantial amount of defense work under direct con-
tracts with the United States Government.
Employees' Retirement Plan
Retirement Plan payments to the Trustee totaled
$2,543,068,36 during 1951. This amount includes payments
aggregating $1,098,584.36 under the IBM Retirement
Plan, and $1,444,484.00 from the Watson Fund for Sup-
plementing the IBM Retirement Plan.
Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan
For the year 1951, the Board of Directors added
$1,518,286.64 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the
IBM Retirement Plan. This amount was determined by
the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by Mr.
Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation under
his present employment contract as compared with his
prior contract and, in accordance with its past practice,
as recommended by Mr. Watson and approved by the
Board of Directors in connection with Mr. Watson's
voluntary reductions for each year 1942 to 1950, inclusive,
the above amount was added to the Watson Fund for
Supplementing the IBM Retirement Plan. The Fund,
after taking into account interest income and the payment

United States Government, Department
of Justice Complaint
On January 21, 1952, the Department of Justice filed a
complaint against IBM alleging that it is a monopoly.
We deny the charge.
The fact is that IBM is one of many business machines
companies in a highly competitive field. Our machines are
used to perform a small percentage of the accounting and
record keeping work of American business and govern-
ment. There are numerous other machines on the market
which are advertised, sold, and used to do the same work
that IBM machines are used to do. To allege that IBM is a
monopoly in view of its position in this competitive field
is to disregard the facts.
IBM has adhered to the highest standards of conform-
ance to all laws and business ethics. We have never em-
ployed any measures to eliminate or restrain competition.
We reject as unfounded any construction of our business
conduct as in violation of the anti-trust laws, and intend
to vindicate our position in the courts.
Research and Development
During 1951 our research and engineering laboratories
worked on a number of important projects for the United
States Government in connection with the Defense Pro-
gram, and also continued their work on projects relating
to new inventions and improvements of our existing
products.
IBM World Trade Corporation
The net assets of IBM World Trade Corporation
(wholly-owned subsidiary) and its subsidiaries located in

Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, Hungary,
Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan Countries continue
to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet pending more
settled conditions in these countries. During the year 1951
the Corporation adjusted its investment in these countries
by $3,323,609.14, representing net income from current
operations remaining in these countries and other invest-
ment adjustments, and, in accordance with its past prac-
tice, added a similar amount to the reserve for foreign
investment. The Corporation has not included in its cur-
rent income account any income from these countries not
actually received in dollars.
Foreign investment in "All Other Countries," stated on
the December 31, 1951, balance sheet at $36,193,354.98,
is comparable to $32,342,476.41 at December 31, 1950,
an increase of $3,850,878.57, the result of the current
year's operations.
During 1951 the IBM World Trade Corporation's Ca-
nadian subsidiary completed the construction of a new
factory building at Toronto, Canada, which added 123,000
square feet of floor space to manufacturing facilities at
that location.
IBM Officers and Directors
At the January 15, 1952 meeting of the Board of Direc-
tors, Mr. Thomas J. Watson, Jr., Executive Vice-President
of the Corporation since 1949, was elected President. Mr.
John G. Phillips, President of the Corporation since 1949,
was elected Vice-Chairman of the Board, and Chairman
of the Executive and Finance Committee. Mr. Thomas J.
Watson, Chairman of the Board of Directors, will continue
as chief executive officer of the Corporation.
Mr. John L. Barton, a member of the Board of Directors
and Resident Manager at the Corporation's Endicott, New
York, plant, retired effective August 1,1951, after fifty-one

===== PAGE 11 =====
years of continuous service with IBM and its predecessor
companies. Mr. Albert L. Williams, Vice-President and
Treasurer, was elected to succeed Mr. Barton on the Board
of Directors.
In Memoriam
The Board of Directors deeply regret the death on
January 22, 1952, of the Hon. Robert P. Patterson, who
was elected to the Board of Directors of the Corporation
in March 1948, and served with distinction on the Board,
the Executive and Finance Committee, and as advisory
general counsel.

will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1952.

INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of International
Business Machines Corporation and domestic subsidiary company as
of December 31, 1951, and the related consolidated income and
earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have also
examined the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation, wholly-
owned subsidiary of International Business Machines Corporation,
for the year ended December 31, 1951. Our examination was made
in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards, and accord-
ingly included such tests of the accounting records and such other
auditing procedures as we considered necessary in the circumstances.
We accepted, after review, available reports of independent auditors
on the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation's foreign subsidia-
ries and branches supplemented by the companies' own statements and
cabled information.
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and
consolidated income and earned surplus account present fairly the
financial position of the companies at December 31, 1951, and the
results of their operations for the year then ended, in conformity with
generally accepted accounting principles applied on a basis consistent
with that of the preceding year. We further certify to the correctness
of the financial information presented in the comparative statement
of operations for the past ten years.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
