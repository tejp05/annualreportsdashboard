To the Stockholders:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1945, together with Consolidated Balance
Sheet of the domestic companies and the Canadian sub-
sidiary company as of December 31, 1945, with the Audi-
tor's Certificate attached.
The results of the year's operations were as follows:
Net Profit Before U. S. Federal and Cana-
dian Income and Excess Profits Taxes.. $36,443,706.60
U. S. Federal and Canadian Income and
Excess Profits Taxes—Estimated 25,550,000.00
Net Profit for the year $10,893,706.60
The net profit of $10,893,706.60 after taxes compares
with $9,711,355.88 for 1944, an increase of $1,182,350.72.
The net profit in 1944 after taxes equalled $8.90 per share
on the 1,091,443 shares then outstanding. In January, 1945
the Company added 54,483 shares through a stock divi-
dend, making a total of 1,145,926 shares, on which the
net profit after taxes for 1945 was $9.51 per share.
The net profit for 1945 of $36,443,706.60 before taxes
compares with $37,711,355.88 for the year 1944.
Included in the total estimated taxes of $25,550,000.00
is $20,150,000.00 Excess Profits Taxes.
The net profit for the year 1945 is after provision for
voluntary renegotiation of war contracts made with the
United States Government computed in the same manner
as was accepted by the Government for the year 1944.

Cash dividends totaling $6,861,000.00 were paid during
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of
$1.50 per share.
In January, 1946 the Corporation's capital stock was
split up by issuing one-quarter additional share for each
share held at the close of business on January 16, 1946,
which resulted in increasing the issued and outstanding
capital stock from 1,145,926 shares to 1,432,407 shares.
The quarterly cash dividend paid March 11, 1946 con-
tinued at the rate of $1.50 per share on the increased
number of shares, representing the equivalent of a 25 per
cent greater cash dividend than before the split-up.
On September 14, 1945, an IBM Retirement Plan was
announced to the Company's employees to become effective
as soon as the necessary approval had been received from
the United States Treasury Department. In announcing
this Plan and the adjustment of rates of pay in the return
of our factories to a forty-hour week, Mr. Watson said, in
part, to our more than 18,000 employees: "The only reason
the Company is doing these things is that you of IBM
deserve them and have earned them. They are the rewards
for the fine service you have given our Company and our
Country." Government approval of the Plan having been
received, the following letter was mailed to employees on
December 31,1945:
December 31,1945
"To each Employee of
International Business Machines Corporation:
"Enclosed please find copy of the details of the IBM
Retirement Plan which I announced on September 14,1945
at Endicott and by broadcast over telephone hookup to
all factories and principal offices. This Plan has been ap-
proved by the Treasury Department of the United States
Government and is now in efi'ect.
"Under the Plan, beginning on an employee's retire-
ment date, and for life thereafter. International Business
Machines Corporation will add to the amount each em-

ployee is entitled to receive as his (or her) Social Security
benefit whatever amount is necessary to provide a monthly
retirement income of from $60.00 per month to $130.00
per month based on length of continuous service.
"Of the total monthly income provided under the Plan,
the amount received through Social Security is paid for
equally by the employee and the Company, but the
amount which is added to Social Security is paid for en-
tirely by the Company.
"We have today made payments for the IBM Retire-
ment Plan amounting to $1,480,000 of which $760,000
represents the first year's premiums to the Equitable Life
Assurance Society of the United States on the present
number of employees, and $720,000 the initial payment
(of an estimated total of $7,200,000) to the Endicott Trust
Company as trustee under a permanent, irrevocable Trust
which cannot be discontinued and which has been created
for the exclusive benefit of employees in order to provide
the full benefits of the IBM Retirement Plan for present
employees from the date of their employment.
"It is our aim to put into effect policies from time to
time which will help to eliminate worry and fear from em-
ployees of the IBM Corporation and their families. In
carrying this out we put into effect a group insurance plan
under which the amount of insurance protection increases
rapidly during the employees' early years of employment
so that their families would receive the maximum protec-
tion when it is most needed. This group insurance will be
carried on until retirement age. Then the employee's re-
tirement income for life, as provided under the IBM Re-
tirement Plan, takes the place of the group insurance
carried for the protection of our employees' dependents.
"The IBM Retirement Plan will not be changed in any
way that would reduce the combined amount of monthly
retirement income each employee will receive from the
Company and from his Social Security benefit as set forth
in the table on page 5 of the Plan unless, and only to the
extent that, the Government should reduce the Social
Security benefits now in effect from age 65.
Sincerely,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President"

For the information of new stockholders, Mr. Watson's
compensation is not based on a bonus or extra compensa-
tion voted by the Board of Directors, but is based on the
principle established by the agreement entered into when
he became head of the Corporation in 1914 that he should
receive a fixed salary plus a percentage having a relation
to profits, after cash dividends, so that he would participate
in the success of the Corporation produced by his efforts
and inventions.
Older stockholders will recall that in the proxy state-
ment which accompanied the notice of annual meeting of
stockholders held in 1943, it was explained that after the
United States entered the war, Mr. Watson had requested
that his new employment contract, which was dated as of
January 1, 1942, should provide that he should not per-
sonally profit by the war activities of the Corporation. It
was further explained that the regular business of the
Corporation had increased substantially during 1942, out-
side of munitions, including business from various de-
partments of the United States Government, and that in
view of the practical difficulty of segregating the amount
of this regular business strictly attributable to war condi-
tions and because of Mr. Watson's desire to make certain
that he would receive no compensation from war business,
he had requested, and the Board of Directors had approved
his request, that his percentage compensation for the year

===== PAGE 9 =====
1942 should not exceed the amount received by him as
percentage compensation for the year 1939, in which year
the Corporation had no war business. The amount paid
Mr. Watson for 1939 was the lowest paid under his
contract for any year since 1937. Similarly, for each of
the years 1943,1944 and 1945 effect was given to the exclu-
sion of war profits from the earnings on which Mr. Watson's
percentage compensation was computed.
The Board determined the amount of the above re-
ductions requested by Mr. Watson to aggregate $1,365,-
582.94 to December 31, 1945 and, adopting Mr. Watson's
suggestion, set aside this amount from the Reserve for
After-War Adjustments and Contingencies for the purpose
of establishing a fund which your Board of Directors de-
cided should be known as the "Watson Fund for Supple-
menting IBM Retirement Plan," the Fund and the income
therefrom to be used as directed by the Board of Directors
to supplement the IBM Retirement Plan.
Under the provisions of the Presidential Proclamation of
September 29, 1945 declaring an end of the emergency
period, the Company charged against its income account
for the year 1945 the amount of $2,980,004.48 representing
the unamortized cost of its emergency facilities acquired
under Certificates of Necessity.
The net assets of the Company and its subsidiaries
located in liberated countries and in other countries where
exchange is blocked continue to be reserved on the Balance
Sheet to $1.00, pending a more accurate determination of
their final recovery value.
The Reserve for After-War Adjustments and Contingen-
cies shown on the Balance Sheet at December 31, 1944 in
the amount of $8,000,000.00 was reduced during 1945 by
$1,365,582.94 representing the amount set aside by the
Board of Directors for the Reserve for Watson Fund Sup-

During 1945 there was invested $19,574,999.77 in rental
machines, factory and office equipment, and land and
buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting
to $3,791,877.69 was written off and charged against re-
serves provided out of prior and current years' earnings.
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in Decem-
ber, 1945 on the Corporation's holdings of 100,000 shares
of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart Manufactur-
ing Company.
In November, 1945 the Company effected a modification
of its Ten-Year Sinking Fund Debentures, due
October 1,1951, which eliminated the provision for Sinking
Fund payments in the amount of $1,500,000.00 per annum,
commencing October 1, 1946.
During the war the Company manufactured 38 different
major munitions products and our Research and Engineer-
ing Departments worked on 99 different important develop-
ment projects for the United States Government. We are
proud to say that our employees delivered all shipments
on schedule, that nothing was rejected because of poor
workmanship, and the average absenteeism rate of our
factory employees was less than three per cent. No em-
ployee lost any time in connection with changes from
manufacture of one munitions product to another, or in
the reconversion of manufacturing to our regular products
when war work was completed. We pay the highest
tribute to our Production Management and to every indi-
vidual employee who participated in our war-time program.

===== PAGE 11 =====
Prior to the end of the War, the men and women of our
main plant at Endicott had received for the fifth time the
Army-Navy production award for outstanding achievement
in the production of war materials and our plant at Pough-
keepsie had received this same award for the fourth time.
The Corporation's transition from war to peacetime
operations was largely effected by the end of the year and
although the production of our factories is already in excess
of what it was prior to the war, our backlog of unfilled
orders at the present time is very substantial.
The termination of the War has made possible acceler-
ated progress in carrying on improvements in the Com-
pany's regular products as well as in new developments.
In addition, long range basic research is being undertaken
for the purpose of discovering and developing new princi-
ples as applied to the Corporation's products.
It is with deep regret that your Board of Directors re-
ports the death of one of its most valued members, Mr.
Charles Smith, on October 8, 1945. Mr. Smith served as
a director of the Corporation from 1919 until his death.
As of V-E Day 32 per cent of our men were serving
in the armed forces of the United States. As of March
18, 1946 we had welcomed the return of 71 per cent of
our men in service and in addition, had employed 455
veterans who had no previous service with the Company.
We extend our deepest sympathy to the families of the
one hundred and fourteen of our men and one woman who
gave their lives or are missing in action.
A Notice, Proxy Statement and form of Proxy for the
Annual Meeting of Stockholders to be held April 30, 1946
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 5,1946.
By Order of the Board of Directors,
April 1,1946

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and domestic and Canadian
subsidiary companies as of December 31, 1945, and the consolidated
income and earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have
reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting procedures
of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed audit of
the transactions, have examined accounting records of the domestic
companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we deemed
appropriate.
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on the
accounts of the Canadian subsidiary and the available financial state-
ments, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by cabled
information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign subsidiaries
and branches. Our examination was made in accordance with gen-
erally accepted auditing standards applicable in the circumstances
and included all procedures which we considered necessary, except
that it was impracticable to obtain confirmation on all accounts due
from the United States Government but we satisfied ourselves by
other means as to the substantial accuracy of such accounts.
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and
related consolidated income and earned surplus account fairly present
the financial condition of the International Business Machines Cor-
poration and domestic and Canadian subsidiary companies at De-
cember 31, 1945 and the results of operations for the year ended on
that date, in conformity with generally accepted accounting principles
applied on a basis consistent with that of the preceding year.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
March 30, 1946
