To THE STOCKHOLDERS:
Your Directors submit for the information of Stockholders
Consolidated Income and Surplus Account for the year
1934, together with Consolidated Balance Sheet as of De-
cember 31, 1934, with the Auditors' Certificate attached.
Net Income for the year 1934, including Foreign Sub-
sidiaries and Branches, before deducting Federal Taxes, but
after providing for full Depreciation ($2,578,352.06), De-
velopment and Patent Expenses ($855,804.90) and Interest
($34,489.45) was $7,522,362.55, compared with $6,651,424.68
for the year 1933, being an increase of $870,937.87.
After deducting estimated Federal Taxes of $925,000.00,
the Net Income for the year was $6,597,362.55 equal to
$9.38 per share on 703,345 shares outstanding December 31,
1934, compared with $5,736,424.68 equal to $8.15 per share
for the year 1933, based on an equal number of shares,
being an increase of $1.23 per share.
Net Current Assets at the end of the year were $2,970,-
761.26, compared with $3,623,918.48 at the end of 1933. The
decrease was caused by the sale of the net current assets
($1,931,266.68) of the Dayton Scale Division to The Hobart
Manufacturing Company, as referred to later on in this
report. If the transfer of this item had not taken place, the
Net Current Assets would have shown an increase of about
$1,278,109.46, compared with December 31, 1933.

===== PAGE 6 =====
The Balance Sheet item of $4,141,566.83 under "Invest-
ments" includes the book value of the net current assets
($1,931,266.68) as referred to above, plant and equip-
ment and patents and goodwill, etc., of the Dayton Scale
Division which were sold and transferred to The Hobart
Manufacturing Company in exchange for 100,000 shares
of the latter's "Class B" capital stock. The sale of the
Dayton Scale Division assets was approved at a Special
Meeting of the Stockholders of the Company held for the
purpose on June 25,1934. A quarterly dividend of $25,000.00
on the Hobart shares was received in December, 1934.
Expenditures totalling $7,491,570.65 were made during
the year for rental machines, plant and equipment and land
and buildings. A total of $3,241,444.64 of dismantled and
obsolete equipment was written off and charged against
reserves. Plant and equipment assets of the Dayton Scale
Division having a gross book value of $2,000,468.90 were
sold.
Regular quarterly cash dividends of $1,50 per share were
paid during the year and in addition a stock dividend of
2 percent was declared in November, payable January 10,
1935, to stockholders of record December 22, 1934. The
necessary additional 14,067 shares, including scrip for frac-
tional shares, have been issued accordingly. The sum of
$453,193.37 has been authorized by your Directors to be
transferred from Surplus to Declared Capital to represent
the book value of this stock dividend.
In the latter part of the year, your Directors authorized
the issuance of Group Life Insurance policies to employees
of one year's service or longer, all premiums to be paid by

===== PAGE 7 =====
the Company. Our employment of factory workers re-
mained steady throughout the year.
During the year, the Company continued its program of
improving existing types of equipment, and has proceeded
with the development of new devices started prior to 1934.
It has also commenced the development of new and addi-
tional devices which appear to offer profitable commercial
possibilities.
By order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have made an examination of the accounts and records relating
to the consolidated balance sheet of the International Business Machines
Corporation and its Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as
at December 31, 1934 and reviewed the consolidated income and surplus
account for the year ended on that date, but we have not made a detailed
audit of all transactions. In that connection, we accepted after review
the company's own financial statements relating to foreign subsidi-
aries and branches. The company's accounts have been consistently
maintained in accordance with accepted accounting principles and all
information and explanations desired were furnished.
The accompanying consolidated balance sheet, based upon such
examination, fairly presents, in our opinion, the financial condition of
the International Business Machines Corporation and its Domestic
and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as at December 31, 1934, and the
related consolidated income and surplus account the results of opera-
tions for the year ended on that date.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
Certified Public Accountants
