To THE STOCKHOLDERS:
Your directors submit for the information of Stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Surplus Account for the
year 1940, together with Consolidated Balance Sheet of
the domestic companies and the Canadian subsidiary-
company as of December 31, 1940, with the Auditor's
Certificate attached.
Net Profit for the year 1940, including foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches, but before deducting estimated
U. S. Federal and Canadian Taxes, was $13,097,013.10,
compared with $11,362,692.27 for the year 1939, being an
increase of $1,734,320.83. As in previous years, a reserve
for the full amount of the Blocked Foreign Net Profits has
been deducted in arriving at the Net Profit for the year
1940.
After deducting estimated U. S. Federal and Canadian
Taxes of $3,666,000.00, including U. S. Excess Profits Tax
of $165,915.00 (in accordance with the Excess Profits Tax
Act of 1940 as amended in 1941), the Net Profit for the
year 1940 was $9,431,013.10, compared with $9,092,692.27
for the year 1939, being an increase of $338,320.83. The
Net Profit for 1940 after taxes equals $10.50 per share on
the 898,178 shares outstanding December 31, 1940, which

===== PAGE 6 =====
shares include 42,770 shares issued as a stock dividend on
April 1, 1940.
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in De-
cember 1940 on the Company's holding of 100,000 shares
of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart Manufac-
turing Company.
An amount of $2,000,000.00 was transfen-ed from
Surplus to Reserve for Contingencies, and the Reserve
for Sales Promotion amounting to $300,000.00 was also
transferred to the Reserve for Contingencies, which reserve
as of December 31, 1940 amounts to $5,300,000.00.
There was invested during 1940 $10,259,539.84 in rental
machines, plant and equipment and land and buildings.
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to $2,415,-
817.23 was written off and charged against reserves pro-
vided out of prior and current years' earnings.
Four quarterly cash dividends of $1.50 per share were
paid during the year, and bonded indebtedness was re-
duced by $2,000,000.00. In addition, a stock dividend of
5% (44,909 shares) was declared in November 1940 and
issued January 30, 1941 to stockholders of record January
15, 1941.
The Company has under construction at its Endicott,
New York, factory an additional building which will
provide about 200,000 square feet of floor space for manu-
facturing facilities.

It is with deep regret that your Directors report the
death of Mr. Oscar L. Gubelman on October 10, 1940.
Mr. Gubelman served as a most valued member of the
Board of Directors and of the Executive and Finance
Committee of the Company from its formation until his
death, and during this long period his ability and deep
interest in the business enabled him to render outstanding
service to the Company.
By order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the Consohdated Balance Sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian
Subsidiary Companies as of December 31, 1940, and the Consohdated
Income and Surplus Account for the fiscal year then ended. In connec-
tion therewith, we have reviewed the system of internal control and
the accounting procedures of the domestic companies and, without
making a detailed audit of the transactions, have examined or tested
accounting records of the domestic companies and other supporting
evidence, by methods and to the extent we deemed appropriate. The
companies' accounts have been maintained in conformity with gen-
erally accepted accounting principles, applied on a basis consistent
with that of the preceding year except as set forth in the note to the
Consolidated Income and Surplus Account.
We have accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors
on the accounts of the Canadian subsidiary and the company's own
financial statements, supplemented by cabled information, on certain
non-consolidated foreign subsidiaries and branches. In those cases
where recent financial statements were not available, the net invest-
ments in foreign subsidiaries were stated, substantially, at December
31, 1939 values after the adjustments noted on the Consolidated
Balance Sheet.
In our opinion, the accompanying Consolidated Balance Sheet and
related Consolidated Income and Surplus Account present fairly the
position of the International Business Machines Corporation and
Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as at December 31,
1940 and the results of operations for the year ended on that date.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
Certified Public Accountants.
