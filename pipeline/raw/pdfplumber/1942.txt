To THE STOCKHOLDERS:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1942, together with Consolidated Balance
Sheet of the domestic companies and the Canadian sub-
sidiary company as of December 31, 1942, with the
Auditor's Certificate attached.
Net Profit for the year 1942, including foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches, but before deducting estimated
U. S. Federal and Canadian Income and Excess Profits
Taxes, was $26,919,755.49 compared with $19,044,633.14
for the year 1941, being an increase of $7,875,122.35
after provision for after-war adjustments in each year.
Net Profit for 1942 after deducting estimated U. S.
Federal and Canadian Income and Excess Profits Taxes
of $18,240,000.00 was $8,679,755.49, compared with $9,-
844,633.14 for the year 1941, a decrease of $1,164,877.65.
This decrease is after the deduction from gross income of
$1,500,000.00 for after-war adjustments (compared with
provision of $500,000.00 for 1941) and after increased
taxes of $9,040,000.00 or 98 per cent over 1941. Included
in the total taxes of $18,240,000.00 is $12,998,500.00
Excess Profits Taxes, after deducting post-war credits of
$1,464,000.00. These credits are carried on the Balance
Sheet under "Other Assets" pending payment in cash
by the government after the war.
The Net Profit for 1942 after taxes equals $8.77 per
share on the 990,116 shares outstanding December 31,

===== PAGE 6 =====
1942, which shares include 47,088 shares issued as a stock
dividend on January 28, 1942.
The Corporation began in 1935 setting up reserves to
protect its profits in blocked exchange countries and later
also its capital investment in enemy-controlled countries.
By 1942 these reserves amounted to $14,677,934.58, com-
pared with a total investment in enemy-controlled and
and other blocked-exchange countries of $14,428,772.40.
After applying to this investment sufficient reserves to
reduce it to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet, an excess of
$249,163.18 remained in the Reserve for Contingencies.
To this excess of $249,163.18 was added $750,836.82 from
Surplus, providing a reserve of $1,000,000.00. In 1941 a
reserve of $500,000.00 was provided for post-war purposes.
With the $1,500,000.00 provided from 1942 income for
the same purpose, a total of $3,000,000.00 is available for
contingencies, and post-war use.
A cash dividend of $175,000.00 was received in De-
cember 1942, on the Corporation's holdings of 100,000
shares of the Class "B" capital stock of the Hobart
Manufacturing Company.
During 1942 there was invested $20,615,292.01 in rental
machines, plant and equipment and land and buildings.
Dismantled and obsolete equipment amounting to $3,112,-
620.50 was written off and charged against reserves pro-
vided out of prior and current years' earnings.
In July 1942, the Corporation acquired the assets of the
Munitions Manufacturing Corporation, having previously
acquired all of its outstanding capital stock.
The Corporation is substantially engaged in the manu-
facture of munitions for the United States Government,
and on December 15, 1942, the Corporation's plant at

===== PAGE 7 =====
Endicott, New York, received the Army-Navy "E" for
"high achievement in the production of war materials."
Our Research and Engineering Departments, in addi-
tion to fulfilling our obligations to the United States Gov-
ernment, are carrying on improvements in our products
as well as developing new inventions for the post-war
period.
Four quarterly cash dividends of $1.50 per share were
paid during the year. In addition, a stock dividend of 5
per cent (49,430 shares) was declared in November, 1942,
and issued January 28, 1943, to stockholders of record
January 14, 1943.
It is with deep regret that your directors report the
death of two of its most valued members, Mr. Samuel M.
Hastings on October 23, 1942, and Mr. Edward Cornell
on November 17, 1942. Mr. Hastings served as a director
of the Corporation from its formation until his death.
Mr. Cornell was general counsel for twenty-nine years, a
member of the Board of Directors since 1924 and a mem-
ber of the Executive and Finance Committee since 1926.
The Corporation is gratified to report that as of this
date 21 per cent of our men are serving in the armed forces
of the United States. Of these men in service, 19 per cent
are commissioned officers.
A notice, proxy statement and form of proxy for the
annual meeting of stockholders to be held April 27, 1943,
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1943.
By Order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the Consolidated Balance Sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and Domestic and Canadian
Subsidiary Companies as of December 31, 1942 and the Consolidated
Income and Earned Surplus Account for the year then ended. We
have reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting
procedures of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed
audit of the transactions, have examined accounting records of the
domestic companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we
deemed appropriate.
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on
the accounts of the Canadian Subsidiary and the available financial
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign sub-
sidiaries and branches located in countries other than enemy con-
trolled and countries where exchange is blocked. Our examination was
made in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards
applicable in the circumstances and included all procedures which we
considered necessary, except that it was impracticable to obtain con-
firmation on all accounts due from the United States Government
but we satisfied ourselves by other means as to the substantial accu-
racy of such accounts.
In our opinion, the accompanying Consolidated Balance Sheet and
related Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account fairly
present the financial condition of the International Business Machines
Corporation and Domestic and Canadian Subsidiary Companies as
at December 31, 1942 and the results of operations for the year
ended on that date, in conformity with generally accepted accounting
principles applied on a basis consistent with that of the preceding year.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
Certified Public Accountants
