To the Stockholders:
During 1964, IBM made many significant ad-
vances in serving the rapidly growing information-
handling requirements of the worldwide market.
The most far reaching of these advances was the
introduction of the IBM System/360 which repre-
sented the most important new product announce-
ment in the Company's history. Since the introduc-
tion of this new computing system less than a year
ago, customer reaction has been excellent, and a rec-
ord number of orders have been received from all
over the world.
The number of countries in which IBM does busi-
ness passed the hundred mark last year. This mile-
stone in our worldwide operations is highlighted in
a special pictorial report beginning on page 18. The
Company's operations outside the United States have
become an increasingly significant part of our total
business. Because of this, financial results have been
reported on a worldwide consolidated basis since
January i, 1964. Figures for prior periods have been
restated accordingly and included in this report is a
10-year comparative statement of operations on a
worldwide basis.

New IBM Tele-processing Systems
The use of IBM Tele-processing networks contin-
ued to grow last year as an increasing number of cus-
tomers were using these communications systems to
transmit information between their computing cen-
ters and widely separated locations.
Last year a large Tele-processing network used by
American Airlines for handling passenger reserva-
tions became fully operational. The system, known
as SABRE, was linked to New York City and other
major cities during 1964, and now serves American
Airlines' passengers from coast to coast. Progress also
continued on the installation of two other airline
reservation networks. Pan American World Airways
began operating its PANAMAC system which eventu-
ally will connect 114 cities on six continents to a
computing center in New York City. Delta Air Lines'
computer-based reservations system also became op-
erational in several cities served by that company and

IBM scientists also developed a new mathematical
technique that may lead to a better understanding of
the readings produced by electrocardiograms. This
technique makes it possible for scientists to calcu-
late accurately the distortion of electrical signals as
they pass from the heart to the body's surface, and
may eventually permit a computing system to deter-
mine the effects some heart abnormalities will pro-
duce in electrocardiograms.
In the industrial field, a mathematical technique
was developed for using IBM computers to deter-
mine the most economical patterns for cutting large,
rectangular sheets of stock, such as glass, lumber, or
sheet metal, into smaller sections to fill incoming
customer orders with a minimum of waste. Until
now, it was extremely difficult to determine the most
economical solution to this type of problem since in
many cases millions of possible patterns for cutting
may exist. Another mathematical technique gives
design engineers a method for more accurately cal-
culating the strength of structural elements in such
things as bridges, buildings, and even machine tools.
SRA Becomes IBM Subsidiary

and business of Science Research Associates, Inc.
SRA, which has its headquarters in Chicago, was
one of the first companies of its kind to pioneer the
development of modern learning systems. Today it
develops and publishes many different types of in-
structional materials for elementary and high schools.
