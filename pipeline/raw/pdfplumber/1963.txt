IBM also continued to broaden its family of
machines which customers use to get information
in and out of data processing systems. The Com-
pany recently introduced the IBM 7770 audio
response unit which enables customers to query
a computer for data by dialing the appropriate
digits on an office telephone, and receive a pre-
recorded voice answer within seconds. A retailer,
for instance, could use this unit to check a cus-
tomer's credit rating while the purchase still was
being made.
Other new machines introduced during the
year can read printed or hand-marked informa-
tion automatically, and transmit data from re-
porting terminals to computer centers located a
distance away.
Three of these new machines utilize optical
scanning techniques to read certain kinds of orig-
inal data so it can be more easily fed into a com-
puter for processing.
The IBM 1282 optical reader card punch can
read printed numbers and handwritten marks on
cards, and then punch this data into the cards so
they can be processed by a computer. The ma-
chine is expected to be useful in speeding the
processing of credit card purchases.
The other two scanning machines are IBM op-
tical mark page readers, which can read marks
penciled in between guide lines on standard let-
ter-size data sheets. One model of the machine
can read the data and then punch it into cards
for processing. The other can read the data direct-
ly into a data processing system at a rate of up to
2,000 data sheets an hour. An insurance com-
pany, for instance, could use this machine to read
the facts on a client's application directly into a
computer for processing without first having to
convert the information into punched card form.

The distribution program is called IMPACT,
short for Inventory Management Program and
Control Techniques, and represents a new method
for using computers to manage inventories scien-
tifically. The program enables wholesalers and
retailers to maintain balanced inventories at the
lowest possible cost. It is being used by a number
of firms, including those in the grocery, drug,
variety store, and automotive parts businesses.
The program for the printing industry was de-
veloped as a result of an extensive study by IBM
and the Oklahoma Publishing Company. It in-
volves the use of a data processing system for
automatically setting the type used in newspa-
pers, books, magazines, and brochures. As textual
material is fed into the computer, it hyphenates
the words and spaces out the lines to column
width. It then creates instructions on paper tape
which the printer's automatic linecasting ma-
chines can use to set the story in type.
Automated Design Engineering is the new
technique for the manufacturing industry. It en-
ables a computer to produce automatically the
engineering designs required to custom-build
transformers, turbines, motors, and other major
electrical products. The computer is able to iden-
tify each step involved in designing an electrical
product, and from specifications fed into it can
produce in minutes the complete design and all
the steps needed to manufacture the product.
The IBM Systems Research Institute last year
continued its vital job of investigating the most
effective ways of using IBM computers. It provided
IBM systems engineers from all parts of the world
with the most up-to-date information on this sub-
ject. It also furthered its investigation of new sys-
tems techniques which may lead to new and
broader use of IBM equipment in the future.

IBM data processing systems are also being

techniques are being developed for analyzing

heart or brain action into a computer for com-
parison with normal patterns which have been
stored in the system's memory. The computer
then prepares an analysis which can be studied
by physicians. The experimental X-ray technique
allows radiologists to convert X-ray photographs
into data which a computer can analyze, store,
and later reproduce in a photographic form that
may enable doctors to make observations not
clearly visible on the original X-ray film.
The growing importance of computers in med-
icine was further underscored last year when the
Center for Health Sciences at the University of
California, Los Angeles, dedicated its powerful
IBM computing installation—the nation's largest
for medical research. Scientists are using this in-
stallation to study the functioning of the human
brain during prolonged space flights, conduct
complex biochemical experiments, and analyze
the various causes associated with heart disease.
