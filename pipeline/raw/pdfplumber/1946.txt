To the Stockholders:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1946, together with Consolidated Balance
Sheet of the domestic companies and the Canadian sub-
sidiary company as of December 31, 1946, with the
Auditor's Certificate attached.
The results of the year's operations were as follows:
Net Profit Before U. S. Federal and Cana-
dian Income Taxes $31,266,433.85
U. S. Federal and Canadian Income
Taxes—Estimated 12,500,000.00
Net Profit for the year $18,766,433.85
The net profit for the year of $18,766,433.85 compares
with $10,893,706.60 for 1945, an increase of $7,872,727.25.
The net profit in 1945 equalled $9.51 per share on the
1,145,926 shares then outstanding. In January 1946, the
Company added 286,481 shares through a stock split-up
by issuing one-quarter additional share for each share
held at the close of business on January 16, 1946, increas-
ing the issued and outstanding capital stock to 1,432,407
shares, on which the net profit for 1946 was $13.10 per
share.
Cash dividends totaling $8,587,414.50 were paid during
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of
$1.50 per share. As cash dividends were continued at the
same rate per share after the stock split-up of January
1946, the amount paid during the year exceeded the
amount paid in 1945 by 25 per cent.

===== PAGE 6 =====
The Company's increased volume of business in its
regular products during 1946 required an investment of
$30,232,688.83 in rental machines, factory and office
equipment, and land and buildings. Dismantled and ob-
solete equipment amounting to $6,539,319.68 was written
off and charged against reserves provided out of prior and
current years' earnings.
As a result of a substantially increased sales organiza-
tion and the continued demand for our products, unfilled
orders at the end of the year were considerably higher
than at any time in the history of the Company.
To provide funds for the growth of the business in addi-
tion to the amount available from re-investment of profits,
in August 1946 the Corporation effected the sale at par
to The Prudential Insurance Company of America of
$30,000,000 Twelve-Year Debentures due August 1,
1958. The proceeds of the sale of these Debentures were
used to refund the $17,000,000 Ten-Year 2^% Debentures
due October 1,1951 and the remainder of the net proceeds,
together with the sale to the same insurance company of
$20,000,000 Eighteen-Year 2^% Debentures due Feb-
ruary 1, 1965, sold at par in February 1947, will provide
additional working capital to be used for increased pro-
duction of rental machines and for other corporate pur-
poses including the construction of an additional building
for manufacturing facilities at the Company's Pough-
keepsie, New York plant, estimated at $6,000,000, in-
cluding equipment. The additional building will provide
approximately 210,000 square feet of floor space for
manufacturing facilities.
The net assets of the Corporation and its subsidiaries
located in Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany,
Hungary, Italy, Japan, Poland, and the Balkan countries

The Corporation's reconversion was completed early in
1946 and, although production was somewhat curtailed
due to the shortage of material, our overall output sub-
stantially surpassed any peacetime year, and will increase
as expanded factory production facilities become available.
"Other Investments" shown on the December 31, 1946
Balance Sheet at $2,536,480.37 includes an additional in-
vestment of $1,060,550.00 during 1946 in the Commercial
Controls Corporation (represented by 11,465 shares of
Preferred Stock and 4,873 shares of Class "A" Stock of
that Company), and an investment of $634,671.48 in Globe
Wireless, Ltd. (represented by 32,511 shares of Class "B"
Capital Stock of that Company), resulting in each case
from the transfer and sale of assets.
A cash dividend of $150,000 was received in December
1946 on the Corporation's holdings of 100,000 shares of the
Class "B" Capital Stock of the Hobart Manufacturing
Company.

During the year, in addition to making improvements
in its existing products, the Company added nine new
products to its regular lines, including an Electronic Mul-
tiplier, the first commercial application of electronics for
calculating. At the present time our research and engineer-
ing laboratories are working on eighty-four different pro-
jects relating to our regular products and new inventions.
It is with deep regret that your Board of Directors
reports the death of Mr. Abraham L. Kellogg, who served
with distinction as a director of the Corporation from 1934
until his death on August 25,1946.
A Notice, Proxy Statement and form of Proxy for the
Annual Meeting of Stockholders to be held April 29, 1947
will be sent to stockholders on or about April 1, 1947.
By Order of the Board of Directors,
THOMAS J. WATSON,
President.

To the Stockholders of the
INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of the Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation and domestic and Canadian
subsidiary companies as of December 31, 1946 and the consolidated
income and earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have
reviewed the system of internal control and the accounting procedures
of the domestic companies and, without making a detailed audit of
the transactions, have examined accounting records of the domestic
companies and other supporting evidence to the extent we deemed
appropriate.
We accepted, after review, the report of independent auditors on
the accounts of the Canadian subsidiary and the available financial
statements, mainly the company's own statements supplemented by
cabled information, on the accounts of non-consolidated foreign
subsidiaries and branches. Our examination was made in accordance
with generally accepted auditing standards applicable in the circum-
stances and included all procedures which we considered necessary.
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and
related consolidated income and earned surplus account fairly present
the financial condition of the International Business Machines Cor-
poration and domestic and Canadian subsidiary companies at De-
cember 31, 1946 and the results of operations for the year ended on
that date, in conformity with generally accepted accounting principles
applied on a basis consistent with that of the preceding year.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
