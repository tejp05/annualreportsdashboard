S O F T W A RE One of the fastest-growing segments
of the information processing industry, software has become a $70-billion-a-year business worldwide.
Expenditures in this area are growing twice as fast as hardware expenditures.
Simply stated, software is a set of instructions that tells computers what to do, and how and when to do it.
Software includes operating systems to control overall operation of the computer, application packages to
direct the computer in solving problems or performing tasks such as payroll or
billing, and other programs for jobs such as data base and network management.
As global competition intensifies, demand is growing for more and more
sophisticated software to harness the power of computers and gain the competitive edge.

In 1990, revenues from information processing services totaled more than $100 billion worldwide.
Industry consultants expect these revenues to more than double by tlie mid-1990s.

F I B ER O P T I CS There simply is no faster, more reliable
or better way today to transmit information—from telephone conversations to computer data and video
services—than through fiber-optic cables. These hair-thin strands of glass or plastic can carry a
hundred times more information than conventional copper wires.
Fiber-optic cables are fast becoming the standard medium for telephone networks and
the basis for an array of developing technologies that will bring new and improved data
services to homes, schools, government agencies and businesses. Among them are multimedia systems,
high-definition television, digital photography and image processing.
Just about any type of information can be transmitted through flexible fiber-optic cables,

===== PAGE 16 =====
including sound, images, audio and video signals, electronic mail and odier digital information.
Lightweight fiber-optic cables are impervious to temperature extremes, corrosion—even electrical noise.
Through fiber-optic connections, companies will be able to realize substantial savings by locating
their storage devices, printers and other equipment several miles away from their computers.
As the ability to rapidly access and transfer information becomes a competitive advantage, the use of fiber-
optic cables is certain to grow.
O P T I C AL S T O R A GE D I S KS Bcst-known
for high-fidelity sounds, the compact disk (CD) is synonymous with more than music to a growing number of
computer users. This small, optical device can store encyclopedias of information. A single disk can hold
as much information as 500 floppy disks.
The optical disks vast storage capacity and durability make it ideally suited for archiving library materials,
storing office files such as insurance claims, or multimedia motion pictures and sounds.
The market for optical storage disks is expected to blossom as advances in technology bring improvements
in data retrieval speed, cost and the ability to change or erase information on a disk.
M U L T I M E D IA T E C H N O L O G I ES Technology opens
doors to opportunity when it is successfully applied to solving customer problems. Multimedia technologies
hold exceptional promise. Blending video, sound, animation and near-photo-quality images with conventional
text and graphics, multimedia makes information appearing on a computer screen
exciting and engaging. It is expanding the scope and impact of personal computers
in schools, businesses and homes.
By combining the entertainment of television with the power of computer-assisted instruction,
multimedia systems can make education on all levels—including business training—more effective. Studies
show that people remember 20 percent of what they hear; 40 percent of what they see and hear;

===== PAGE 17 =====
and 75 percent of what they see, hear and do. Multimedia technologies capture attention and encourage
participation in the learning process. They also make subjects such as science, math and reading as interesting
as video games and musical television.
With multimedia, business meetings can take place through video conferences from desktop computers.
Sales presentations can be made using full-motion video and stereophonic sound. Electronic mail can be
expanded to include voice messages, animated diagrams and video clips.

puters could account for as much as 25 percent of worldwide sales of personal computers by 1995,

Other technologies that are making computers easier to use include voice recognition, touch screens,
artificial intelligence and gesture recognition.
