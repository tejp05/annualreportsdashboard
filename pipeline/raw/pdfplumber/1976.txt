IBM's operations, with very minor exceptions, are in the field of
information-handling systems, equipment and services to solve the
increasingly complex problems of business, government, sci-

other areas of human activity. IBM's products include data

An Expanding Usefulness...
In 1976, more people accomplished more productive work with
IBM computers and office products than ever before. They were
people in almost all walks of life-from the corner druggist to
the large pharmaceutical manufacturer, from the small trucker to
the big railroad, from the small-town bank to the international
financial institution.
This growth in productivity resulted from the creative use by
customers of IBM products designed to meet information-handling
needs of almost any kind or scale. In the office, new equipment
and programming enabled customers to add the computer's
resources to the streamlining of paperwork through word
processing. Small-scale computers as powerful as large systems
of the 1960s were available at far lower cost. Larger computers
became faster and more versatile.
For systems of all sizes, customers were able to choose from
a wider variety of central processor speeds, storage capabilities,
terminals, input-output devices, and program products-
selecting the combinations best suited for the work to be done.
In addition, the use customers made of these resources grew more
flexible. Some enlarged their central installations. Others turned
to distributed computing-installing small computers and terminals
in remote locations, such as branch offices, to handle local needs,
as well as to communicate with the central system.
In many ways and in many industries, IBM customers were
innovative in creating new applications. Increasingly, they used
computer systems to help manage their enterprises. By analyz-
ing and controlling operations on a current basis, they achieved
new levels of efficiency.
The following pages illustrate the widening usefulness to
customers of IBM computers and office products in 1976-picturing
applications in all parts of the world, as well as showing new
IBM products and research efforts that promise still greater pro-
ductivity for customers in the years ahead.
