Electric Typewriter Division:
IBM entered the dictation equipment field last year with a new family of office
machines designed to help create a more effective Executive-Secretary team. The new
machines, known as the IBM EXECUTARY line of dictating equipment, are being
manufactured and sold by the Electric Typewriter Division.
IBM EXECUTARY dictation machines feature a 4-inch-wide magnetic belt that
allows an executive to review his dictation whenever he wishes, and revise it by
simply re-recording over the unwanted material. After a secretary has transcribed the
dictation, she can completely erase the belt so it can be used again.
Three models of fully-transistorized IBM EXECUTARY equipment are now available,
including a dictation unit, a transcriber, and a combination unit with both dictating
and transcribing features. The division expects to broaden this line of machines
eventually to include portable and remote models.
Sales of all divisional products, including electric typewriters, electronic typing
calculators and supplies rose to another record high last year.
An important advance in IBM typewriter supply technology was made in i960
with the introduction of a plastic ribbon which produces typewritten characters
which are extremely well defined.
IBM's electric typewriters, the new EXECUTARY line of dictation equipment, and
the 632 typing calculator that combines electronic calculating with an IBM electric
typewriter in a single unit, are produced at the division's manufacturing plant and
development laboratory in Lexington, Kentucky.

The Service Bureau Carporation:
The Service Bureau Corporation is a wholly-owned, but independently operated sub-
sidiary of IBM that is supplying a growing list of companies with data processing serv-

SBC offices throughout the country are equipped to handle a customer's data proc-

SBC's major business is the preparation of repetitive reports such as payrolls and

These sharply-focused rays, which are produced by a device called a maser, will be
capable of carrying vast amounts of information. As a result, they show promise in
space communication and navigation, data processing, radar, the control of chemical
reactions and many other fields.
Solid-state computers of the future will use complex components that are much
smaller than the transistors and other parts used in today's machines. IBM scientists
have developed a new technique that can "grow" these minute components from a
vapor containing the required materials. The technique may eventually be used to pro-
duce entire computer circuits composed of several different kinds of these advanced
components.
IBM is developing a variety of advanced memory devices which can store great
quantities of information and make this data available at speeds of up to billionths of
a second. These memories consist of very high-speed magnetic cores, cryogenic planes,
magnetic thin films, tunnel diodes, and photoscopic materials.
During i960, for example, IBM scientists discovered a method for duplicating
cryogenic thin film memory planes the size of postage stamps, using automatic con-
trol techniques. These tiny circuits, which operate in liquid helium baths where the
temperature approaches 450 degrees below zero, may someday be used in computers
to perform unusually fast arithmetic, logic and switching functions.
A photoscopic memory was also developed last year which can "remember" two
million Russian words. This memory unit, which consists of a flexible film disk
small enough to fit into a business envelope, shows promise for IBM's new solid-state
Mark II language translating machine which can render Russian into English. Work
is progressing on the translation of other foreign languages including French and
Chinese.
Other important areas of IBM research include the study of magnetics, luminescent
materials, crystal structures, microwaves, automatic abstracting of printed articles,
advanced machine languages used to instruct computers, the theory of machines, the
use of machines in communication, and the relationship between machines and men.
The spectrum of IBM research activities is being constantly expanded in order to
provide the scientific and engineering knowledge necessary to maintain the Corpora-
tion's technological leadership in the marketplace.

IBM's total amount of long-term indebtedness, all of which is held by The Pru-

On January 4, i960, Prudential exercised its option to convert its $115 million

Stock Option Plan:
Under the terms of the Stock Option Plan which share owners approved in 1956,
the Corporation's officers and other key employees may be granted options to purchase
IBM stock at 100% of the market price on the day the option is granted.
This stock may not be purchased until two years after the option is granted, and
purchases can only be made in installments extending over the following eight years.
The number of shares which may be purchased is adjusted to allow for stock splits
and dividends declared after the option is granted, in accordance with the Plan.
During i960, options were granted for 11,629 shares, and terminated for 892
shares. A total of 7,566 shares were purchased during the year, for which IBM received
$1,199,026. This amount was credited to the capital stock account.
As of December 31, i960, 76 executives held options for 114,644 shares, of which
14,672 shares had become available for purchase. Option prices ranged from $137.70
to I588.50 per share, adjusted for stock dividends and split-ups. A balance of 15,359
shares remained available for future option grants.
Stock Purchase Plan for IBM Employees:
The IBM Employees 1958 Stock Purchase Plan, adopted by share owners on April
29, 1958, was extended by the Board of Directors to be effective for the third plan
year starting July i, i960.
All regular employees who have completed one year's continuous service, and are
not participants in the Stock Option Plan, may purchase IBM stock through payroll
deductions not exceeding 10% of their regular rate of pay. Employees can use these
deductions to buy IBM shares at a price 15% below the market price on the day the
stock is purchased.

IBM World Trade Carporation:
IBM's growing business in the 91 countries it serves outside the United States is con-
ducted by a wholly-owned subsidiary, the IBM World Trade Corporation.
World Trade's branches and subsidiaries employ more than 34,000 people, and
operate 18 manufacturing plants, 6 laboratories and 284 sales offices in all parts of the
Free World.
IBM's international operations were strengthened in many different ways last year.
World Trade continued to consolidate the production of IBM products in its most
suitably located European plants, for example, in order to take advantage of the
internal tariff reductions taking place among the nations within each of Europe's two
regional marketing areas.
World Trade also enlarged its facilities by completing a new manufacturing plant
in Argentina, and expanding its plants in Scotland, France and Japan. Construction
was started on new laboratory buildings in France, Holland and Germany, and a new
office building in West Berlin.
During the year, in response to the growing needs of small business firms abroad,
World Trade introduced the low-priced IBM 3000 punched card data processing sys-
tem. Developed entirely within World Trade laboratories, it uses a card approximately
one-half the size of a standard IBM card.
IBM World Trade also extended its business at the other end of the data processing
spectrum. Orders for medium and large-scale data processing systems increased at a
substantially higher rate than in previous years. In addition. World Trade began pro-
duction in Europe of IBM'S 140I and 7070 solid-state systems to meet growing
demands abroad.
IBM's investment in and advances to the IBM World Trade Corporation rose to
$83,562,594 last year from $57,255,603 in 1959 as a result of the year's transactions,
including an additional IBM loan of $17,500,000 to World Trade. This loan was made
to assist in financing production required by the increased overseas demand for IBM
products. The 20-year loan carries a 5 % interest rate.
The Company's equity in the IBM World Trade Corporation on December 31, i960,
including retained earnings, totaled $286,404,828, compared with $223,588,323 a
year earlier.
