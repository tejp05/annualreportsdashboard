To the Stockholders:
Your directors submit for the information of stock-
holders Consolidated Income and Earned Surplus Account
for the year 1953 of the Corporation and subsidiary com-
panies, Consolidated Balance Sheet of the domestic com-
panies as of December 31, 1953, the Auditor's Certificate,
and a Comparative Statement of Operations for the Past
Ten Years.
The results of the year's operations were as follows:
Net Income
Net Income before U. S. Federal
Income and Excess Profits
Taxes $92,319,210.21
U. S. Federal Income and Excess
Profits Taxes—estimated 58,200,000.00
Net Income for the year $34,119,210.21
The Net Income for the year of $92,319,210.21 before
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes compares
with $78,474,541.37 for 1952, an increase of $13,844,668.84.
U. S. Federal Income and Excess Profits Taxes for the
year 1953 of $58,200,000.00 (which includes Excess Profits
Tax in the amount of $10,000,000.00) compares with
$48,600,000.00 for 1952, an increase of $9,600,000.00.
The Net Income for the year of $34,119,210.21 compares
with $29,874,541.37 in 1952, an increase of $4,244,668.84.
The Net Income in 1953 equalled $10.67 per share on
the 3,198,868 shares of capital stock outstanding at the
end of the year, and compares with $9.34 in 1952, if com-
puted on the same number of shares.

Dividends
Cash dividends totaling $12,779,015.00 were paid during
the year, representing four quarterly cash dividends of
$1.00 per share.
In addition to cash dividends, your Board of Directors
have declared stock dividends over the years because of
the desirability of reinvesting a substantial portion of
earnings in the business to aid in financing its continuing
growth. Stock dividends paid in the past ten years, and
other data, are shown in the Comparative Statement of
Operations which appears on pages 14 and 15.
On January 29,1953, a total of 152,227.40 shares of cap-
ital stock was issued in payment of the stock dividend of
5% declared on October 28, 1952, and $28,923,206.00,
representing $190.00 for each share so issued, was trans-
ferred from Earned Surplus to Capital Stock account.
The Board of Directors on October 27, 1953 declared
a stock dividend payable on January 28, 1954 to
stockholders of record at the close of business on January
4, 1954.
The quarterly cash dividend payable March 10, 1954,
at the rate of $1.00 per share on the increased number of
shares, is the one hundred and fifty-sixth consecutive
quarterly cash dividend paid by the Company.
Rental Machines, Plant and Equipment
During the year 1953, the Corporation's increased
volume of business and replacement of obsolete equipment
required an investment of $122,221,975.64 in rental
machines and parts, factory and office equipment, and land
and buildings. Dismantled and obsolete equipment
amounting to $34,828,020.45 was written off and charged
against reserves provided out of prior and current years'
earnings. At December 31, 1953, the balances of the
Corporation's reserves for depreciation and amortization
of rental machines and parts, factory and office equipment,
and land and buildings aggregated $214,110,344.89.

===== PAGE 7 =====
Construction is expected to be completed this fall of a
research laboratory at Poughkeepsie, New York, which
will add 179,000 square feet of additional floor space to
the Corporation's existing laboratory facilities at that
location.
Nearing completion at Greencastle, Indiana, is a new
manufacturing plant which will provide 180,000 square
feet of floor space.
Construction is also under way of a new building which
will add 139,200 square feet of floor space to existing
manufacturing facilities at Endicott, New York.
In addition to the above new plant and laboratory
facilities, the Corporation has acquired land at Kingston,
New York, to be used as an additional plant site for the
performance of a Government contract. A necessity
certificate has been granted the Corporation by the
Government for this facility.
Long-Term Indebtedness
On November 19, 1953 the Corporation took down an
additional $40,000,000.00 of the $115,000,000.00 included
in the Corporation's agreement of January 22, 1952 with
The Prudential Insurance Company of America. As
previously reported to stockholders, under this agreement
the Corporation will borrow from The Prudential Insur-
ance Company a total of $115,000,000.00 against the
Corporation's 3^% notes, to mature January 1, 2052.
Notes in the amount of $80,000,000.00 have been issued
to date, and the remaining $35,000,000.00 of notes will be
issued in 1954. There are no sinking fund requirements,
but optional prepayments may be made without premium
from available funds arising out of operations or from the
sale of stock. After January 1, 1960, at the option of
either the Corporation or Prudential, the notes may be
converted as a whole but not in part into 25-year 33^%
notes, with provision for equal annual prepayments, with-

together with the Corporation's other long-term indebted-

000,000.00 at December 31, 1953, on which the average

Unfilled Orders
Although more of the Corporation's products were pro-
duced in 1953 than in any year in the history of the
business, unfilled orders at the end of the year were higher
than at the end of any previous year. In addition to the
continued demand for the Corporation's regular products,
the Corporation continues to be engaged in a substantial
amount of defense work under direct contracts with the
United States Government.
Employees' Retirement Plan
For the information of new stockholders, employees
retired after ten or more years of service are provided a
retirement income under the IBM Retirement Plan, sup-
plemented by the Watson Fund for Supplementing the
IBM Retirement Plan.
The Board of Directors revised retirement benefits
December 1, 1953, increasing the minimum retirement
income after ten years of continuous service to $85.00 per
month, including Social Security, as compared with the
previous minimum of $75.00, including Social Security,
and increasing the maximum retirement income after
forty-five years of continuous service to $250.00 per month
including Social Security, from the previous maximum of
$240.00. Retirement income, as heretofore, is based
solely on length of continuous service, regardless of an
employee's earnings or position with the Company. The
amended plan was approved by the Bureau of Internal
Revenue.

Retirement Plan payments to the Trustee totaled
$3,642,070.92 during 1953. This amount includes pay-

Watson Fund for Supplementing IBM Retirement Plan
For the year 1953, the Board of Directors added
$1,976,381.70 to the Watson Fund for Supplementing the
IBM Retirement Plan. This amount was determined by
the Board to be the voluntary reduction made by Mr.
Thomas J. Watson in his percentage compensation under
his employment contract in effect in 1953. In accordance
with its past practice, as recommended by Mr. Watson
and approved by the Board of Directors in connection
with Mr. Watson's voluntary reductions for each year
1942 to 1952 inclusive, the above amount was added to
the Watson Fund for Supplementing the IBM Retirement
Plan. The Fund, after taking into account interest
income and the payment of $1,572,737.00 to the Trustee,
previously mentioned, had a balance at December 31,1953
of $5,462,352.03.
United States Government, Department
of Justice Complaint
The Corporation's lawyers have prepared and filed an
answer to the Government's anti-trust complaint insti-
tuted against the Corporation on January 21, 1952, and
have continued their preparations for the trial. A judge
has been appointed before whom pre-trial conferences and
procedures will be held, and who will conduct the trial
itself.
The Corporation continues to reject as unfounded any
construction of its business conduct as in violation of the
anti-trust laws.

The net assets of IBM World Trade Corporation
(wholly-owned subsidiary) and its subsidiaries located in

Austria, Czechoslovakia, France, Germany, Hungary,
Italy, Japan, Poland and the Balkan Countries continue
to be reserved to $1.00 on the Balance Sheet. During the

Foreign investment in "All Other Countries," stated on
the December 31, 1953 balance sheet at $41,529,517.86,

operations.
On July 3, 1953, IBM World Trade Corporation's
Canadian subsidiary. International Business Machines
Company, Limited, issued and sold its 4^% Series "B"
Notes due July 1,1973, in the aggregate principal amount
of $5,000,000.00, to seven Canadian insurance companies,
at 100% of such principal amount. These notes, together
with the 43^% Series "A" Notes, due October 1, 1972,
brought the total long-term indebtedness of the Canadian
company to $9,000,000.00 at December 31, 1953. Inter-
national Business Machines Corporation unconditionally
guaranteed payment in full of the interest and principal.
On January 26, 1954, the Canadian company's new
factory addition at Toronto, Ontario, was dedicated, pro-
viding an additional 200,000 square feet of floor space at
that location.
Research and Development
Our research and engineering laboratories are continu-
ing their work on a number of important defense projects
for the United States Government and on new and im-
proved commercial products.

===== PAGE 11 =====
New products added to the IBM regular line in 1953
include an electronic calculator of increased capacity for
commercial applications; an accumulating reproducer
which performs combination operations not heretofore
possible; an electronic calculator embodying the principle
of storing information on a magnetic drum; a desk model
card punch; a printing summary punch; an electric type-
writer specifically designed for the preparation of telephone
toll statements; new time equipment including a radio su-
pervised time control system, the master clock of which
is controlled by the hourly radio broadcasting of a time
signal provided by the U. S. Bureau of Standards; and toll
recording equipment which greatly facilitates the hand-
ling and control of charges and records for toll highways.
In 1953 the Corporation also developed the second of a
series of IBM Electronic Data Processing Machines. The
new equipment is a digital-alphabetical system of great
flexibility, designed to streamline accounting and record-
keeping procedures in commerce and industry.

INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS MACHINES CORPORATION:
We have examined the consolidated balance sheet of International
Business Machines Corporation and domestic subsidiary company as
of December 31, 1953, and the related consoHdated income and
earned surplus account for the year then ended. We have also
examined the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation, wholly-
owned subsidiary of International Business Machines Corporation,
for the year ended December 31, 1953. Our examination was made
in accordance with generally accepted auditing standards, and accord-
ingly included such tests of the accounting records and such other
auditing procedures as we considered necessary in the circumstances.
We accepted, after review, available reports of independent auditors
on the accounts of IBM World Trade Corporation's foreign subsidia-
ries and branches supplemented by the companies' own statements and
cabled information.
In our opinion, the accompanying consolidated balance sheet and
consolidated income and earned surplus account present fairly the
financial position of the companies at December 31, 1953, and the
results of their operations for the year then ended, in conformity with
generally accepted accounting principles applied on a basis consistent
with that of the preceding year. We further certify to the correctness
of the financial information presented in the comparative statement
of operations for the past ten years.
R. G. RANKIN & CO.
